>[!info]- Markov decision process
>A Markov decision process
>$$
>\mathcal{M}=\{ \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{T}, r \}
>$$
>where 
>- $\mathcal{S}$ is the state space
>- $\mathcal{A}$ is the action space
>- $\mathcal{T}$ is the state-action transition operator
>- $r: \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A}\to \mathbb{R}$ is the reward function
>
>**Partially observed Markov decision process**:
>$$
>\mathcal{M}=\{ \mathcal{S, A, O, T, E}, r \}
>$$
>where 
>$\mathcal{O}$ is the observation space
>$\mathcal{E}$ is the emission probability $p(o_t|s_t)$

## The goal of reinforcement learning

Let $\pi$ be a policy based on a set of learnable parameters $\theta$.

> Find the parameters $\theta$ that defines our policy so as to maximize the expected rewards under the trajectory distribution

$$
\theta^*=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_\theta\quad \mathbf{E}_{\tau \sim p_{\theta}(\tau)}\left[ \sum_tr(\mathbf{s_t}, \mathbf{a}_t) \right] 
$$
This can be rewritten as
$$
\theta^*=\sum_t \mathbf{E}_{(\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{a}_t)\sim p_{\theta}(\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{a}_t)}[r(\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{a}_t)]
$$
![[Pasted image 20260213143847.png]]

$$\underbrace{ 
p_\theta(\mathbf{s}_1, \mathbf{a}_1, \dots, \mathbf{s}_T, \mathbf{a}_T) }_{ P_\theta(\tau) }= p(\mathbf{s}_1)\prod_{t=1}\underbrace{ T \pi_\theta(\mathbf{a}_t|\mathbf{s}_t)p(\mathbf{s}_{t+1}|\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{a}_t) }_{ \text{Markov chain on }(\mathbf{s,a}) }
$$

### Infinite horizon case
If $T=\infty$

If $p(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{a}_t)$ converge to a stationary distribution (system is ergodic and aperiodic)
$$
\mu=\mathcal{T}\mu \implies (\mathcal{T}-\mathbf{I})\mu=0
$$
$\mu$ is eigenvector of $\mathcal{T}$ with eigenvalue 1

$\implies$ $\mu=p_\theta(\mathbf{s,a})$ (stationary distribution).

**Insight**: The initial steps define less and less of the average reward, giving rise to:
$$
\underbrace{ \theta^{\star}=\arg \max _\theta E_{(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{a}) \sim p_\theta(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{a})}[r(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{a})]  }_{ \text{Infinite horizon case} }\quad \quad \underbrace{ \theta^{\star}=\arg \max _\theta \sum_{t=1}^T E_{\left(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{a}_t\right) \sim p_\theta\left(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{a}_t\right)}\left[r\left(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{a}_t\right)\right] }_{ \text{Finite horizon case} }
$$

## Value function
Rewrite
$$
E_{\tau \sim p_\theta(\tau)}\left[\sum_{t=1}^T r\left(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{a}_t\right)\right]=E_{\mathbf{s}_1 \sim p\left(\mathbf{s}_1\right)}\left[E_{\mathbf{a}_1 \sim \pi\left(\mathbf{a}_1 \mid \mathbf{s}_1\right)}\left[Q\left(\mathbf{s}_1, \mathbf{a}_1\right) \mid \mathbf{s}_1\right]\right]
$$
where
$$
Q\left(\mathbf{s}_1, \mathbf{a}_1\right)=r\left(\mathbf{s}_1, \mathbf{a}_1\right)+E_{\mathbf{s}_2 \sim p\left(\mathbf{s}_2 \mid \mathbf{s}_1, \mathbf{a}_1\right)}\left[E_{\mathbf{a}_2 \sim \pi\left(\mathbf{a}_2 \mid \mathbf{s}_2\right)}\left[r\left(\mathbf{s}_2, \mathbf{a}_2\right)+\ldots \mid \mathbf{s}_2\right] \mid \mathbf{s}_1, \mathbf{a}_1\right]
$$
Thus, we define a *Q-function*:
$$
Q^\pi\left(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{a}_t\right)=\sum_{t^{\prime}=t}^T E_{\pi_\theta}\left[r\left(\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}, \mathbf{a}_{t^{\prime}}\right) \mid \mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{a}_t\right] \text { : total reward from taking } \mathbf{a}_t \text { in } \mathbf{s}_t
$$
and, related, a *value function*:
$$
V^\pi\left(\mathbf{s}_t\right)=\sum_{t^{\prime}=t}^T E_{\pi_\theta}\left[r\left(\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}, \mathbf{a}_{t^{\prime}}\right) \mid \mathbf{s}_t\right]: \text { total reward from } \mathbf{s}_t=E_{\mathbf{a}_t \sim \pi\left(\mathbf{a}_t \mid \mathbf{s}_t\right)}\left[Q^\pi\left(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{a}_t\right)\right]
$$
### Utilization of Q-functions and value function
- If we have a policy $\pi$, and we know $Q^\pi(\mathbf{s,a})$ we can improve $\pi$.
- Compute gradient to increase probability of a good action $\mathbf{a}$: if $Q^\pi(\mathbf{s,a})>V^\pi(\mathbf{s})$, then $\mathbf{a}$ is better than average.

## Types of algorithms
- **Policy gradients**: directly differentiate the argmax of the expected reward
- **Value-based**: estimate value function or Q-function of the optimal policy (no explicit policy)
- **Actor-critic**: estimate value function or Q-function of the current policy, use it to improve policy
- **Model-based RL**: estimate the transition model, and then...
	- Use it for planning (no explicit policy)
	- Use it to improve a policy
	- Something else

### Trade-offs
No single best method; choice depends on constraints (Efficiency vs. Stability). High sample efficiency often comes at the cost of stability and complexity.

- **Sample Efficiency**: _Off-policy_ (Q-learning, Model-based) reuses data (high efficiency) vs. _On-policy_ (Policy Gradient) needs fresh data (low efficiency).
- **Computation**: If simulation is cheap (e.g., chess), sample inefficiency is acceptable for better stability/simplicity.
- **Stability**: _Policy Gradient_ optimizes the true objective (stable); _Value-based_ methods use fixed-point iteration (can diverge with Neural Nets).
- **Assumptions**: Algorithms differ on handling stochasticity, continuous vs. discrete actions, and full observability.

## Discount factors
For infinite time-horizon settings rewards might become infinite.

Trick: Better to get rewards sooner than later.

$$
y_{i,t}\approx \text{direct reward}+\gamma \text{ future rewards}
$$
where $\gamma \in[0,1]$ is the *discount factor* (often between 0.9 and 0.99).
