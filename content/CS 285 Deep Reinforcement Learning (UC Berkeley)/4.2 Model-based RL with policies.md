Idea: Instead commit to a *policy*.

**Naive approach: Back propagate directly through policy (v. 2.0)**
- Will suffer from vanishing/exploding gradients
- Unlike LSTM, we can't just "choose" simple dynamics, dynamics are chosen by nature

Solution: Use derivative-free ("model-free") RL algorithms with the model used to generate synthetic samples. Essentially "model-based acceleration" for model-free RL.

## Dyna-style algorithms

**Dyna algorithm**: Online $Q$-learning algorithm that performs model-free RL with a model.

**General "Dyna-style" recipe:**
1. Collect some data, consisting of transitions $(s,a,s',r)$
2. Learn model $\hat{p}(s'|s,a)$ (and optionally, $\hat{r}(s,a)$)
3. Repeat $K$ times:
	4. Sample $s\sim \mathcal{B}$ from buffer
	5. Choose action $a$ (from $\mathcal{B}$, from $\pi$, or random)
	6. Simulate $s'\sim \hat{p}(s'|s,a)$ (and $r=\hat{r}(s,a)$)
	7. Train on $(s,a,s',r)$ with model-free $RL$
	8. (Optional) take $N$ more model-based steps
