## Double $Q$-learning
Regular $Q$-learning suffers from overestimation from the $Q$-value due to the combination of the $\max_{\mathbf{a}'}$ operation and noise in the untrained $Q$ function. 

Double $Q$-learning combats this by **de-correlating** the action selection from the action evaluation:
- **Action Selection:** Use the current/live network $Q_\phi$ to find the best action.
- **Action Evaluation:** Use the target network $Q_{\phi'}$ to evaluate that specific action.

## Multi-step ($N$-step) returns
Early in training, the Q-function is random/poor, meaning the target values are mostly useless noise. During this phase, the primary learning signal comes entirely from the single-step reward $r$.

We  incorporate multiple steps of real reward before bootstrapping with the $Q$-value, learning to lower bias, but higher variance.
$$
Y_{n\text{-step}} = \sum_{t'=t}^{t+n-1} \gamma^{t'-t} r_{t'} + \gamma^n \max_{a'} Q_{\phi'}(s_{t+n}, a')
$$
### Off-Policy Problem with N-Step Returns
Standard 1-step Q-learning is strictly off-policy. However, N-step returns are only technically valid for on-policy data.

**Solutions:**
1. **Ignore it:** Often works surprisingly well in practice.
2. **Dynamic Trace Cutting:** Dynamically truncate $N$ to the point where the buffer's actions diverge from your current deterministic greedy policy.
3. **Importance Sampling:** Construct a stochastic policy and weight the $N$-step returns using importance sampling.
