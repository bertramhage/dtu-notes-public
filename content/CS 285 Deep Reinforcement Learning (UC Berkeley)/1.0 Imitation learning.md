>[!info]+ **Changing notation**
>Similar to supervised learning let the policy $\pi_\theta(\mathbf{a}_t|\mathbf{o}_t)$ dictate the action based on the observation $\mathbf{o}_t$, where $t$ is *discrete* (although in the real world $t$ might be continuous).
>
>Let the policy be *stochastic* outputting a *distribution* of actions where a deterministic policy be a special case were one discrete action has a probability of 1.0.
>
>Let  $\mathbf{s}_t$ denote the state at time $t$. $\pi_\theta(\mathbf{a}_t|\mathbf{s}_t)$ is a policy based on a fully observed state. Knowing the state carries all the information of the observation, but we *might* not know the state solely based on the observation.

## Supervised learning in non i.i.d. scenarios (behavioral cloning)
**Autonomous driving example**:
![[Pasted image 20260206142112.png]]
**Supervised learning**:
$\mathbf{o}_t, \mathbf{a}_t\to$ training data $\to$ supervised learning (behavioral cloning) $\pi(\mathbf{a}_t|\mathbf{o}_t)$

Problem: i.i.d. property is violated since small deviations in predictions $\mathbf{a}_t$ affects $\mathbf{o}_{t+1}$
![[Pasted image 20260206142559.png|300]]


#### Bounding the total mistakes
The objective is (MLE):
$$
\max_\theta \mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{o}_t\sim p_{\text{data}(\mathbf{o}_t)}}[\log \pi_\theta(\mathbf{a}_t|\mathbf{o}_t)]
$$
we test under $p_{\pi_\theta}(\mathbf{o}_t)$, $p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{o}_t)\neq p_{\pi_\theta}(\mathbf{o}_t)$ (distribution shift)

Define a cost $c$
$$
c(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{a}_t)=\begin{cases}
0 \text{ if }\mathbf{a}_t=\pi^*(\mathbf{s}_t) \\
1 \text{ otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$
ie. is 0 if it matches the human action.

Goal: $\min \mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{s}_t\sim p_{\pi_\theta}(\mathbf{s}_t)}[c(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{a}_t)]$. "Will we minimize the number of mistakes the policy runs when we run it".

>[!help] Upper bound derivation
>Assume: $\pi_\theta(\mathbf{a}\neq \pi^*(\mathbf{s})|\mathbf{s})\le \epsilon$ for all $\mathbf{s} \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$
>
>Probability of making a mistake ($T$ is full time horizon):
>$$
>\underbrace{ \mathbf{E}\left[ \sum_t c(\mathbf{s}_t|\mathbf{a}_t) \right] }_{ O(\epsilon T^{2}) } \le \epsilon T+(1-\epsilon)(\epsilon(T-1)+(1-\epsilon)(\dots)) 
>$$

**More general**:
Assume $\pi_\theta(\mathbf{a}\neq \pi^*(\mathbf{s})|\mathbf{s})\le \epsilon$, for $\mathbf{s}\sim p_{\text{train}}(\mathbf{s})$.

>[!help] Upper bound derivation
>Probability of *not* making a mistake:
>$$
>p_\theta(\mathbf{s}_t)=(1-\epsilon)^{t}p_{\text{train}}(\mathbf{s}_t)+(1-(1-\epsilon)^t)p_{\text{mistake}}(\mathbf{s}_t)
>$$
>from here we can derive that
>
>$$
>\sum_t\mathbf{E}_{p_\theta(\mathbf{s}_t)}[c_t]=\sum_t\sum_{\mathbf{s}_t}\le \sum_t\epsilon + 2\epsilon t\le \epsilon T+2\epsilon T^{2} \implies O(\epsilon T^{2})
>$$

#### Recovering from mistakes
The above bound assumes no recovery from mistakes. In reality we can *often* recover from mistakes.

## Addressing problems with behavioral cloning
There are *multiple* ways to handle the issues in behavioral cloning described above

#### Augment training data with mistakes
- Intentionally add *mistakes* and *corrections*
- The mistakes hurt but the correction help, often more than the mistakes hurt

#### Use more powerful models that very few mistakes
- Approach: minimize $\epsilon$, such that even under the quadratic complexity, the total value is still low.

###### Using the whole history
- To mitigate the non-markovian property: use models that can handle histories (eg. Transformers, LSTM, ...)
- This can introduce problems with auxiliary signals

###### Multimodal behavior
- Problem: The expert 50% of the time goes left and 50% of the time goes right => average: goes straight (eg. using Gaussian distribution)
![[Pasted image 20260206151857.png|300]]
Solution: Use more expressive continuous distributions
- Gaussian mixture models ![[Pasted image 20260206152611.png|200]]
- Latent variable models![[Pasted image 20260206152655.png|300]]
- Diffusion models![[Pasted image 20260206152723.png]

$$
\begin{align}
x_0=\text{ true image} \\
x_{i+1}=x_{i} + \text{ noise} \\
\text{Learned network} = f(x_i)=x_{i-1} \\
\Leftrightarrow  \\
\mathbf{a}_{t,0}= \text{ true action} \\
\mathbf{a}_{t, i+1}=\mathbf{a}_{t,i}+ \text{ noise} \\
\text{Learned network}: f(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{a}_{t,i})=\mathbf{a}_{t,i-1}
\end{align}
$$

###### Multitask learning
Increase the number of reached states by expanding the end goals to multiple tasks at train time, i.e. learn $\pi(\mathbf{a}|\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{g})$ ($\mathbf{g}$= goal state), maximize $\log \pi_\theta(\mathbf{a}^i_t|\mathbf{s}^i_t, \mathbf{g}=\mathbf{s}^i_t)$.

Going beyond just imitation – Online self improvement:
![[Pasted image 20260206154612.png]]


###### DAgger algorithm
Goal make $p_\text{data}(\mathbf{o}_t)=p_{\pi_\theta}(\mathbf{o}_t)$, instead of being clever about the policy, be clever about the algorithm

1. train $\pi_\theta\left(\mathbf{a}_t \mid \mathbf{o}_t\right)$ from human data $\mathcal{D}=\left\{\mathbf{o}_1, \mathbf{a}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{o}_N, \mathbf{a}_N\right\}$
2. run $\pi_\theta\left(\mathbf{a}_t \mid \mathbf{o}_t\right)$ to get dataset $\mathcal{D}_\pi=\left\{\mathbf{o}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{o}_M\right\}$
3. Ask human to label $\mathcal{D}_\pi$ with actions $\mathbf{a}_t$
4. Aggregate: $\mathcal{D} \leftarrow \mathcal{D} \cup \mathcal{D}_\pi$

Improves the bound complexity to a *linear* combination of $T$.

## Remaining problems with imitation learning
Even with above optimization, still issues remains with imitation learning (motivation for RL):
- Human needs to provide data which is typically finite, where deep learning works best when data is plentiful
- Humans are not good at providing some kinds of actions
- Human can learn autonomously; can our machines do the same?
