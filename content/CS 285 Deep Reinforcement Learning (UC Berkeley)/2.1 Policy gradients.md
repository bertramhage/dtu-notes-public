For the objective
$$
\theta^*=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_\theta \mathbf{E}_{\tau \sim p_\theta(\tau)}\left[ \sum_tr(\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{a}_t) \right] =\operatorname*{arg\,max}_\theta J(\theta).
$$
Assuming we can "run" the policy in the real world $N$ times (correspinding to sampling from $p(\mathbf{s_1})$ and $p(\mathbf{s}_{t+1}|\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{a}_t)$):
$$
J(\theta)=\mathbf{E}_{\tau \sim p_{\theta}(\tau)}\approx \frac{1}{N}\sum_i\sum_tr(\mathbf{s}_{i,t}, \mathbf{a}_{i,t})
$$
>[!help] Gradient of $J(\theta)$
>We can derive that
>$$
>\begin{aligned}
>\nabla_\theta J(\theta)& \\
>=&\mathbf{E}_{\tau \sim p_\theta(\tau)}\left[ \left( \sum_{t=1}^T \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(\mathbf{a}_t,\mathbf{s}_t)\right) \left( \sum_{t=1}^{T} r(\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{a}_t) \,  \right)  \right]  \\
>\approx& \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \left( \sum_{t=1}^{T} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(\mathbf{a}_{i,t}|\mathbf{s}_{i,t}) \right)\left( \sum_{t=1}^{T} r(\mathbf{s}_{i,t}, \mathbf{a}_{i,t}) \,  \right) \,  \, 
>\end{aligned}
>$$

The **Policy gradient** algorithms utilizes
$$
\theta \leftarrow\theta \alpha \nabla_\theta J(\theta)
$$
where $\alpha$ is the step size

>[!info] The REINFORCE algorithm
>1. sample $\left\{\tau^i\right\}$ from $\pi_\theta\left(\mathbf{a}_t \mid \mathbf{s}_t\right)$ (run the policy)
>2. $\nabla_\theta J(\theta) \approx \sum_i\left(\sum_t \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta\left(\mathbf{a}_t^i \mid \mathbf{s}_t^i\right)\right)\left(\sum_t r\left(\mathbf{s}_t^i, \mathbf{a}_t^i\right)\right)$
>3. $\theta \leftarrow \theta+ \alpha \nabla_\theta J(\theta)$
>4. repeat

## Policy gradients in practice
One can think of the policy gradient optimization as a sort of $r$-weighted "MLE". Thus a formal definition of "trial and error".

### Continuous example
For a $\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t) = \mathcal{N}(f_\theta(s_t), \Sigma)$ the $\log \pi$ probability becomes
$$\log \pi(a_t|s_t) \propto -\frac{1}{2}(f_\theta(s_t) - a_t)^T \Sigma^{-1} (f_\theta(s_t) - a_t)$$
Gradient is computed by backpropagating the error term (difference between mean output and sampled action) through the network.

## Reducing variance in policy gradient optimization
Adding a constant to rewards (e.g., $r + C$) does not change the optimal policy but significantly affects the gradient variance with finite samples.

This can be mitigated using two techniques:
1. The policy at time $t'$ cannot affect rewards at time $t$ if $t < t'$. **Future actions do not change past rewards**. We leverage this:
$$\nabla J(\theta) \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{t=0}^T \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_{i,t}|s_{i,t})\hat{Q}_{i,t},\quad \hat{Q}_{i,t} = \sum_{t'=t}^T r(s_{i,t'}, a_{i,t'})$$
2. Introduce a **baseline** $b$ such that only rewards higher than $b$ are upweighted (does not introduce a bias)
$$\nabla J(\theta) \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(\tau_i) [R(\tau_i) - b]$$
	- Optimal bias: $b^* = \frac{E[g(\tau)^2 R(\tau)]}{E[g(\tau)^2]}$

## Off-policy policy gradients
In the REINFORCE algorithm we need to create **new** samples for every gradient step. NN GD takes small steps => computational cost / inefficient.

The off-policy solution "reuses" data (eg. sampled from another/previous policy) such that
$$J(\theta') = E_{\tau \sim \bar{p}(\tau)} \left[ \left( \prod_{t=1}^T \frac{\pi_{\theta'}(a_t|s_t)}{\bar{\pi}(a_t|s_t)} \right) \sum_{t=1}^T r(s_t, a_t) \right]$$
$$\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}} J\left(\theta^{\prime}\right) \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{t=1}^T \frac{\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{s}_{i, t}, \mathbf{a}_{i, t}\right)}{\pi_\theta\left(\mathbf{s}_{i, t}, \mathbf{a}_{i, t}\right)} \nabla_{\theta^{\prime}} \log \pi_{\theta^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{a}_{i, t} \mid \mathbf{s}_{i, t}\right) \hat{Q}_{i, t}$$
which we can approximate (using the crude assumption that $\frac{p_{\theta'}(\mathbf{s}_t)}{\bar{p}(\mathbf{s}_t)}=1$ to simplify to
$$
\nabla_{\theta'} J(\theta') \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{t=1}^T \left( \frac{\pi_{\theta'}(a_{i,t}|s_{i,t})}{\bar{\pi}(a_{i,t}|s_{i,t})} \right) \nabla_{\theta'} \log \pi_{\theta'}(a_{i,t}|s_{i,t}) \hat{Q}_{i,t}
$$

## Implementing policy gradients
To make the gradient computation more efficient we implement a "pseudo-loss" as a weighted maximum likelihood.


$$
\tilde{J}(\theta) \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{t=1}^T \log \pi_\theta\left(\mathbf{a}_{i, t} \mid \mathbf{s}_{i, t}\right) \hat{Q}_{i, t}
$$
Policy gradient pseudo-code:**
```
# Given:
# actions - (N*T) x Da tensor of actions
# states - (N*T) x Ds tensor of states
# q_values - (N*T) x 1 tensor of estimated state-action values
# Build the graph:
logits = policy.predictions(states) # This should return (N*T) x Da tensor of action logits
negative_likelihoods = tf.nn.softmax_cross_entropy_with_logits(labels=actions, logits=logits)
weighted_negative_likelihoods = tf.multiply(negative_likelihoods, q_values)
loss = tf.reduce_mean(weighted_negative_likelihoods)
gradients = loss.gradients(loss, variables)
```
