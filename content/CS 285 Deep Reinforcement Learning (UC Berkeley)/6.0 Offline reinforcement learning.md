Objective: Given dataset $\mathcal{D}$ learn the *best possible* policy $\pi_\theta$.

Formally:
$\mathcal{D}=\{ (\mathbf{s}_i, \mathbf{a}_i, \mathbf{s}'_i, r_i) \}$
$s\sim d^{\pi_\beta}(\mathbf{s})$
$\mathbf{a}\sim \pi_\beta(\mathbf{a}|\mathbf{s})$ (generally not known)
$\mathbf{s}'\sim p(\mathbf{s}'|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})$
$r\leftarrow r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})$

RL objective: $\max_\pi \sum_{t=0}^{T} \mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{s}_t\sim d^\pi(\mathbf{s}),\mathbf{a}_t\sim \pi(\mathbf{a}|\mathbf{s})}[\gamma^tr(\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{a}_t)] \,$

![[Pasted image 20260330123350.png|400]]

ie.:
- Find the "good stuff" in a dataset full of good and bad behaviors
- Generalization: Good behaviour in one place may suggest good behavior in another place
- "Stitching": parts of good behaviors can be recombined

## Distribution shift
Naive offline reinforcement learning (RL) suffers from distribution shift because the algorithm actively seeks out actions that the model overestimates, pulling the evaluation distribution away from the training distribution.

Thus, existing challenges with sampling error and function approximation error in standard RL become much more severe in offline RL:

![[Pasted image 20260330123849.png|400]]

Objective: 
$$
\min_Q\mathbf{E}_{(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{a})\sim \pi_\beta(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})}[(Q(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})-y(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a}))^{2}]
$$

Expect good accuracy when $\pi_\beta(\mathbf{a}|\mathbf{s})=\pi_{\text{new}}(\mathbf{a}|\mathbf{s})$
Even worse: $\pi_\text{new}=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_\pi \mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{a}\sim \pi(\mathbf{a|\mathbf{s}})}[Q(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})]$

## Explicit policy constraint methods
1. $Q(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})\leftarrow r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})+\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{a}'\sim \pi_\text{new}}[Q(\mathbf{s}',\mathbf{a}')]$
2. $\pi_\text{new}(\mathbf{a|s})=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_\pi \mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{a}\sim \pi(\mathbf{a|s})}[Q(\mathbf{s,a})] \text{ s.t. }D_{\text{KL}}(\pi\mid\mid \pi_\beta)\le\epsilon$
3. repeat...

- Easty to implement
- Constrains $\pi_\text{new}$

**Alternative constraint**:
- $\pi(\mathbf{a|\mathbf{s}})\ge0$ only if $\pi_\beta(\mathbf{a|\mathbf{s}})\ge\epsilon$
- Significantly more complex to implement
- Closer to what we want

## Implicit policy constraints
Avoid directly querying out-of-distribution actions by framing the policy improvement step as an advantage-weighted behavioral cloning problem
$$
\theta \leftarrow \arg \max_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{a} \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ \log \pi_{\theta}(\mathbf{a}|\mathbf{s}) \exp \left( \frac{1}{\alpha} \hat{A}(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{a}) \right) \right]
$$

## Conservative $Q$-learning (CQL)
$$
\hat{Q}^{\pi} = \arg\min_{Q} \max_{\mu} , \alpha E_{\mathbf{s} \sim D,, \mathbf{a} \sim \mu(\mathbf{a}|\mathbf{s})}[Q(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{a})] - \alpha E_{(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a}) \sim D}[Q(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{a})]
$$
$$
+ E_{(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{s}') \sim D}\left[\left(Q(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{a}) - \left(r(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{a}) + E_{\pi}[Q(\mathbf{s}', \mathbf{a}')]\right)\right)^2\right]
$$

Explicitly penalize the Q-values of unseen/OoD actions to prevent overestimation, creating a provable lower-bound on the true Q-function.
