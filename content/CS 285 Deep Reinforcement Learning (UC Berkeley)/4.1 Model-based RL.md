## Naive approach (v. 0.5)

1. Run base policy $\pi_0(\mathbf{a}_t,\mathbf{s}_t)$ (eg. random policy) to collect $\mathcal{D}=\{ (\mathbf{s,a,s}')_i \}$
2. Learn dynamics model $f(\mathbf{s,a})$ to minimize $\sum_i\mid\mid f(\mathbf{s}_i,\mathbf{a}_i)-\mathbf{s}_i'\mid\mid^{2}$
3. Plan through $f(\mathbf{s,a})$ to choose actions

- How systems identification works in classical robotics
- Some care should be taken to design a good policy
- **Doesn't work well with deep neural-networks**

## "DAGGER for models" (v. 1.0)

1. Run base policy $\pi_0(\mathbf{a}_t,\mathbf{s}_t)$ (eg. random policy) to collect $\mathcal{D}=\{ (\mathbf{s,a,s}')_i \}$
2. Learn dynamics model $f(\mathbf{s,a})$ to minimize $\sum_i\mid\mid f(\mathbf{s}_i,\mathbf{a}_i)-\mathbf{s}_i'\mid\mid^{2}$
3. Plan through $f(\mathbf{s,a})$ to choose actions
4. Execute those actions and add the resulting data $\{ (\mathbf{s,a,s}')_j \}$ to $\mathcal{D}$

## MPC-based approach (v. 1.5)
1. Run base policy $\pi_0(\mathbf{a}_t,\mathbf{s}_t)$ (eg. random policy) to collect $\mathcal{D}=\{ (\mathbf{s,a,s}')_i \}$
2. Learn dynamics model $f(\mathbf{s,a})$ to minimize $\sum_i\mid\mid f(\mathbf{s}_i,\mathbf{a}_i)-\mathbf{s}_i'\mid\mid^{2}$
3. Plan through $f(\mathbf{s,a})$ to choose actions
4. Execute the first planned action, observe resulting state $\mathbf{s}'$ (Model-predictive control, MPC)
5. Append ($\mathbf{s,a,s}'$) to dataset $\mathcal{D}$

- Repeat inner loop 3,4,5, and outer loop 2,3,4,5 every $N$ steps
 - The more you replan (3.) the less perfect each individual plan needs to be
 - Can use shorter horizon (3.)

## Model-based RL with uncertainty estimation
Intuition: Only take actions for which we think we'll get high rewards *in expectation* (w.r.t. uncertainty dynamics).

![[Pasted image 20260316094708.png|400]]
Goal: assess the epistemic (/model-) uncertainty:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Estimate: }&p(\theta|\mathcal{D}) \\
\text{Predict: }&\int p(\mathbf{s}_{t+1}|\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{a}_t, \theta)p(\theta|\mathcal{D})\quad \mathrm{d}\theta
\end{aligned}
$$
**Bayesian neural networks**
Common approximation:
$$
\begin{aligned}
p(\theta|\mathcal{D})&=\prod_{i}p(\theta_i|\mathcal{D}) \\
p(\theta_i|\mathcal{D})&=\mathcal{N}(\mu_i,\sigma_i)  \, 
\end{aligned}
$$
**Bootstrap ensembles**
Train $N$ models. $\theta_i$ is trained on $\mathcal{D}_i$ sampled with replacement from $D$.
$$
\int p(\mathbf{s}_{t+1}|\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{a}_t, \theta)p(\theta|\mathcal{D}) \, d\theta \approx \frac{1}{N}\sum_ip(\mathbf{s}_{t+1}|\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{a}_t,\theta_i) 
$$

Resampling with replacement is usually unnecessary because $SDG$ and random initialization makes the models sufficiently independent.

### Planning with uncertainty
$$
\begin{align}
&\text{Before: } J(\mathbf{a}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{a}_H) = \sum_{t=1}^{H} r(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{a}_t), \text{ where } \mathbf{s}_{t+1} = f(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{a}_t) \\
&\text{Now: } J(\mathbf{a}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{a}_H) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{t=1}^{H} r(\mathbf{s}_{t,i}, \mathbf{a}_t), \text{ where } \mathbf{s}_{t+1,i} = f_i(\mathbf{s}_{t,i}, \mathbf{a}_t)
\end{align}
$$
**Other options**:
- Moment matching
- More complex posterior estimation with BNNs
- etc.

## State space (latent space) models
Under high dimensionality, redundancy and/or partial observability:
Separately learn $p(\mathbf{o}_t|\mathbf{s}_t)$ and $p(\mathbf{s}_{t+1}|\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{a}_t)$ (and $p(r_t|\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{a}_t)$).

![[Pasted image 20260316095721.png|400]]
Many choices for approximate posterior $q_\psi(\mathbf{s}_t|\mathbf{o}_{1:t},\mathbf{a}_{1:t})$:
- $q_\psi(\mathbf{s}_t,\mathbf{s}_{t+1}|\mathbf{o}_{1:T}, \mathbf{a}_{1:T})$ - Full smoothing posterior
- $q_\psi(\mathbf{s}_t|\mathbf{o}_t)$ - Single-step, simple but least accurate

Simple special case: $q(\mathbf{s}_t|\mathbf{o}_t)$ is *deterministic*:
$$
q_{\psi}(\mathbf{s}_t|\mathbf{o}_t)=\delta(\mathbf{s}_t=g_\psi(\mathbf{o}_t))\implies \mathbf{s}_t=g_{\psi}(\mathbf{o}_t)
$$
$$
\max_{\phi,\psi} \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \log p_\phi(g_{\psi}(\mathbf{o}_{t+1,i})|g_\psi(\mathbf{o}_{t,i}), \mathbf{a}_{t,i})+\log p_{\phi}(\mathbf{o}_{t,i}|g_\psi(\mathbf{o}_{t,i})) \,  \, 
$$
Everything is differentiable, can train with backprop.
