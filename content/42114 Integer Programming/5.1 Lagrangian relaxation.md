## Lagrangian relaxation

For a IP problem
$$
\begin{align}
\max & cx \\
\text{s.t.} & Dx\le d \\
 & x \in X
\end{align}
$$
drop the constraint $Dx\le d$ and add a penalty term to the objective function:
$$
\begin{align}
\max &\quad cx+u(d - Dx) \\
\text{s.t.} &\quad x \in X
\end{align}
$$
where $u\ge0$ is called the Lagrange multiplier (a konstant).
- If $Dx > d$ the constraint is violated and there is a penalty in the objective
- If $Dx ≤d$ the constraint is satisfied and there is a reward in the objective.

Notice:
- $u\to \infty$: the objective is ignored.
- $u=0$ the constraints are ignored
- Somewhere in between is better

>[!example]+ Uncapacitated facility location
>$y_{ij}:$ fraction of demand of client $i$ satisfied by depot $j$
>$x_j$: 1 if depot $j$ is opened, 0 otherwise
>$$
>\begin{align}
>z = & \max &\quad \sum_{i\in M}^{}\sum_{j\in N}c_{ij}y_{ij}-\sum_{j \in N}f_jx_j &\\
>& \text{s.t.}&\quad \sum_{j \in N}y_{ij}=1  \, & i \in M \\
>&&y_{ij}\le x_j&i \in M, j \in N \\
>&&0\le y_{ij}\le 1 & i \in M, j\in N \\
>&& x_j\in \left\{ 0,1 \right\} & j \in N
\end{align}
>$$
>**Lagrangian relaxation**
>$$
>\begin{align}
>z(u) = &\quad \max &\quad \sum_{i \in M}\sum_{j\in N}c_{ij}y_{ij}-\sum_{j\in N}f_jx_j-\sum_{i\in M}u_i\left( \sum_{j \in N}y_{ij}-1 \right)  \\
>& \text{s.t.} &\quad y_{ij}\le x_{j}\quad i \in M, j \in N \\
>&& 0\le y_{ij}\le 1\quad i \in M, j \in N \\
>&& x_j \in \left\{ 0,1 \right\}\quad j \in N
\end{align}
>$$
>Rewriting the objective function
>$$
>=\sum_{i \in M}\sum_{j \in N}(c_{ij}-u_i)y_{ij}-\sum_{j \in N}f_{j}x_j+\sum_{i \in M}u_i
>$$
>Solving the Lagrangian relaxation:
>- $c_{ij}-u_i$ is the "Lagrangian" profit
>- $\sum_{i \in M}u_i$ is a constant
>- For each facility:
>$$
>z_j(u)=\max\left[ 0,\sum_{i \in M}\max[0,(c_{ij}-u_i)]-f_j \right] 
>$$

## Lagrangian Duality
The best Lagrangian relaxation is the one with the lowest upper bound. This is also called the Lagrangian dual. It can be view as an optimisation problem:
$$
W_{LD}=\min_{u\ge 0}\max_{x \in X}cx + u(d-Dx).
$$
Let $\bar{x}$ denote the solution to 
$$
\begin{align}
\max &\quad cx+u(d-Dx) \\
\text{s.t.} &\quad x \in X
\end{align}
$$
where $u\ge_0$.

We get the **dual bound** $c\bar{x}+u(d-Dx)$.

But if $\bar{x}$ is feasible ($D\bar{x}\le d$):
- We get a primat bound $c\bar{x}$
- The optimal solution $x^*$ lies in the range 
$$
c\bar{x}\le cx^*\le c\bar{x}+u(d- D\bar{x})
$$
- Because $u\ge 0$ and $D\bar{x}\le d$, the gap is closed if
	- $u(d-D \bar{x})=0$, which implies
	- $(D\bar{x})_i=d_i$ whenever $u\ge0$
	- i.e., constraints with a nonzero penalty are tight

### Solving the Lagrangian Dual
$$
W_{LD}=\min_{u\ge 0}z(u)=\min_{u\ge 0} \max_{x \in X}cx+u(d-Dx)
$$
Assume for now
- $X$ is finite: $X=\left\{ x^1,x^2,\dots,x^T \right\}$
- $u$ is a single variable, and not a vector
Then we get a straight line for each point $x^t \in X$
