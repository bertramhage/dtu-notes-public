A strong valid inequality is one that defines a boundary of the integer feasible region (the convex hull) as closely as possible.

## Quality of inequalities

### Dominance
>[!info] Definition of Dominance
>An inequality $\pi x ≤ \pi_0$ **dominates** another inequality $\mu x\le\mu_0$ if there exists a multiplier $u>0$ such that ALL following conditions are met:
>1. The coefficients are tighter (or equal): $u\mu\le\pi$
>2. The right-hand side is tighter (or equal): $\pi_0\le u\mu_0$
>3. They are not identical (one is strictly better)

**Example**:
Consider
$$
\begin{align}
2x_1+4x_2\le 9\quad (1) \\
x_1+3x_2\le 4\quad (2)
\end{align}
$$
If we multiply $(1)$ by $u=\frac{1}{2}$ we get:
$$
x_1+2x_2\le 4.5
$$
satisfying 1., 2., and 3., from the definition – $(2)$ **dominates** $(1)$.

### Redundancy
Redundancy is "collective dominance".

>[!info] Definition of Redundancy
>An inequality is **redundant** if it is dominated by a linear combination of other valid inequalities. Mathematically:
>$$\exists k \geq 2\text{ valid ineq. } \pi^i x \leq \pi_0^i,\quad i=1, \ldots, k$$
>and weights
>$$u_i>0,\quad i=1, \ldots, k$$
> such that 
> $$\left(\sum_{i=1}^k u_i \pi^i\right) x \leq \sum_{i=1}^k u_i \pi_0^i \text{ dominates }  \pi x \leq \pi_0$$

**Example:**
Consider:
$$
\begin{align}
6x_1-x_2\le 9\quad (1) \\
9x_1-5x_2\le 6\quad (2) \\
5x_1-2x_2\le 6\quad (3)
\end{align}
$$
We take $\frac{1}{3}$ of $(1)$ and $\frac{1}{3}$ of $(2)$ we get
$$
2x_1-\frac{1}{3}x_2+3x_1-\frac{5}{3}x_2\le 3+2\Leftrightarrow 5x_1-2x_2\le 5
$$
which dominates $(3)$.

### Facets
>[!info] Definition of Facets
>For a polyhedron $P\in\mathbb{R}^n$ it is **full-dimensional** if its dimension is $n$.
>
>A **face** $F$ is a "slice" of $P$, created by turning an *inequality* $\pi x\le\pi_0$ into an *equality* $\pi x=\pi_{0}$.
>
>A face is a **facet** if $\text{dim}(F)=\text{dim(P)-1}$.

**Example:**

- $x_1+x_2\le 4$: touches at $(2,2)\implies \text{dim}=0\implies$ not facet
- $x_1+2x_2\le 6$: line segment $(0,3) \text{ to }(2,2)\implies \text{dim}=1\implies$facet 

## Valid inequalities for Knapsack problems

>[!info] A minimal cover
>For a knapsack constraint a **cover** $C$ is a set of items whose total weight *exceeds* the capacity $b$.
>
>A cover is **minimal** if removing any single item makes the remaining items fit in the knapsack:
>$$
>\forall k \in C: \sum_{j \in C \backslash\{k\}} a_j \leq b
>$$
>This leads to the **cover inequality**:
>$$
>\sum_{j \in C} x_j\le |C|-1  \, 
>$$


### Separation algorithm
For a knapsack problem with LP-relaxation solution $x^*: 0 ≤ x^*_j\le_1, \text{ for } \forall j$, we wish to find the most violated cover of the IP problem, equivalent to
$$
\sum_{j \in C}x^*_j>|C|-1 \Leftrightarrow 
1>\sum_{j \in C}(1-x^*_j)
$$
$$
\begin{align}
\min & \sum(1-x^*_j)z_j & \text{(where }z_j=1\text{ if item }j \text{ is in }C) \\
s.t. & \sum_{j}a_jz_j\ge b
\end{align}
$$
#### Lifting (Strengthening the Cut)
We can strengthen the inequality $\sum_{j \in C} x_j \le |C|-1$ by adding variables $x_k$ (where $k \notin C$) with a coefficient $\alpha_k$.

- **New Form:** $\sum_{j \in C} x_j + \mathbf{\alpha_k} x_k \le |C|-1$
    
- **Algorithm to find $\alpha_k$:**
    1. **Calculate Remaining Capacity:** Assume $x_k=1$. Remaining capacity is $b' = b - w_k$.
    2. Solve Sub-problem: Find $z$, the maximum number of items from the original cover $C$ that fit into $b'$.
$$z = \max \{ \sum_{j \in C} y_j : \sum_{j \in C} w_j y_j \le b', y \in \{0,1\} \}$$
    3. **Compute Coefficient:** $\alpha_k = (|C|-1) - z$.
    
**Example**:
Consider
$X=\left\{ X\in\mathbb{B}^6 45x_1+46x_2+79x_3+54x_4+53x_5+125x_6\le{17}8 \right\}$ and the fractional point $x^*=\left( 0,0, \frac{3}{4}, \frac{1}{2}, 1,0 \right)$. The cover separation problem becomes:
$$
\begin{align}
\min&\quad  1z_1+1z_2+\frac{1}{4}z_3+\frac{1}{2}z_4+0z_5+1z_6 \\
s.t.&\quad  45z_1+46z_2+79z_3+54z_4+53z_5+125z_6\ge 179 \\
&\quad z \in \mathbb{B}^6
\end{align}
$$
and has the optimal solution $z^R=(0,0,1,1,1,0)$ with optimal value $\frac{3}{4}$. So the cover inequality
$$
x_3+x_4+x_5\le 2
$$
is violated by $1-\frac{3}{4}=\frac{1}{4}$.

**Lifting**
To strengthen this, we can check if variables _not_ in the cover (specifically $x_1$, $x_2$, or $x_6$) can be "lifted" into the inequality with a coefficient $\alpha_k > 0$.

Let's lift **$x_6$** (weight $w_6 = 125$) into the inequality.

1. Calculate Remaining Capacity ($b'$):
    Assume $x_6 = 1$. The original capacity is $b = 178$.
    $$b' = 178 - 125 = 53$$
2. Solve Sub-problem ($z$):
    We need to find the maximum number of items from our original cover $C=\{3, 4, 5\}$ that fit into the remaining capacity of $53$.
    - Weights in $C$: $w_3=79$, $w_4=54$, $w_5=53$.
    - Can we fit $w_3$ (79)? No.
    - Can we fit $w_4$ (54)? No.
    - Can we fit $w_5$ (53)? Yes, exactly.
    The maximum number of items we can pack is **1** (specifically item 5). Thus, $z=1$.
    
3. Compute Coefficient ($\alpha_6$):
    The original right-hand side was $|C|-1 = 3-1 = 2$.
    $$\alpha_6 = (|C|-1) - z = 2 - 1 = 1$$
Result:
We can add $x_6$ with a coefficient of 1. The strengthened inequality becomes:
$$x_3 + x_4 + x_5 + \mathbf{1}x_6 \le 2$$
