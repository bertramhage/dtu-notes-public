>[!info] Cutting plane
>Given an Integer Linear Programming problem IP and an optimal solution $x^*$ fo the LP-relaxation of the IP then a **cutting plane** (or simply a **cut**) is a valid inequality
>$$
>\pi x \le \pi_0\quad \text{ where }\quad  \pi x^*>\pi_0
>$$

>[!info] Cutting plane algorithm
>- Suppose $X=P\cap \mathbb{Z}^n$ and suppose that we know a family $F$ of valid inequalities $\pi x ≤ \pi_0,\quad (\pi,\pi_0) \in F$.
>- Given a specific objective function one is not really interested in finding the complete convex hull, but only an approximation in the area around the optimal solution.
>
>See [[#Cutting plane algorithm]]

## Valid inequalities

We use the insight:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{LHS}\le b \text{ and } \text{LHS}\in\mathbb{Z} & \implies \text{LHS}\le \lfloor b \rfloor  \\
\text{LHS}\ge b \text{ and } \text{LHS}\in\mathbb{Z} & \implies \text{LHS}\ge \lceil b \rceil 
\end{aligned}
$$
How to find $\text{LHS}$?:
- Add together constraints
- Multiply / divide constraints by constant

>[!example]- Valid inequalitites
>For a IP problem:
>$$
>\begin{array}{cl}\operatorname{maximize} & x_1+x_2 \\ \text { subject to } & -2 x_1+2 x_2 \geq 1 \\ & -16 x_1+20 x_2 \leq 26 \\ & x_1, x_2 \geq 0, \text { integer }\end{array}
>$$

>$$
>\begin{aligned}-2 x_1+2 x_2 & \geq 1 \\ -x_1+x_2 & \geq \frac{1}{2} \\ -x_1+x_2 & \geq\left\lceil\frac{1}{2}\right\rceil=1\end{aligned}

## Chvatal Gomory Cuts

Considering the IP model:
$$
\begin{aligned} \operatorname{maximize} & \sum_{j=1}^n c_j x_j \\ \text { subject to } & \sum_{j=1}^n a_{1 j} x_j \leq b_1 \\ & \vdots \\ & \sum_{j=1}^n a_{m j} x_j \leq b_m \\ & x_j \in \mathbb{Z}_{+}, \quad j=1, \ldots, n\end{aligned}
$$
1. Take a linear combination of the constraints using $(u_1,\dots,u_m)\ge0$
$$
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left( \sum_{i=1}^{m} u_ia_{ij} \,  \right) x_j\le \left( \sum_{i=1}^{m} u_ib_i \,  \right)   \, 
$$
$$
\Leftrightarrow \sum_{j=1}^{n} a'_jx_j\le b' \, 
$$
2. Since $x\ge0$ implies $\sum_{j=1}^{n} (a'_j-\lfloor a'_j \rfloor)x_j\ge0 \,$ we have
$$
\sum_{j=1}^{n} \lfloor a'_j \rfloor x_j\le b' \, 
$$
3. Since $x_j \in \mathbb{Z}_+$ implies $\lfloor a'_j \rfloor x_j \in \mathbb{Z}$ we get
$$
\sum_{j=1}^n\lfloor a'_j \rfloor x_j\le \lfloor b' \rfloor 
$$
>[!info] Chvatal Gomory Cuts
>Let 
>$$
>X=\left\{ x:Ax\le b,x \in \mathbb{Z}_+^n \right\} 
>$$
>Every valid inequality for $X$ can be obtained by applying the Chatal-Gomory procedure a finite number of times.
>- Facet constraints can be generated as Chvatal-Gomory cuts.
>- No constructive (polynomial) algorithm for how the linear combination of constraints should be chosen.
>- In practice, the derivation of Chvatal-Gomory cuts must rely on specific features of a given application.

## Gomory cut
In solving an IP problem
$$
\begin{aligned} \max & c x \\ & A x=b \\ & x \geq 0, x \in \mathbb{Z}^n\end{aligned}
$$
denote the optimal basis of the LP-relaxation $B$.

The $i$'th row in the optimal Simplex tableau now expresses the following equation
$$
x_{B_i}+\sum_{j \notin B}a_{ij}x_j=\beta_i
$$
in which $\alpha_{ij}$ and $\beta_i$ are the coefficients in the $i$'th row of the tableau.

If not all RHS in the Simplex tableau are integer continue:

Construct an inequality, which is satiesfied by all solutions to the IP as follows:
- consider a row $i$ in which $\beta_i$ is non-integer
- separate $\alpha_{ij}$ and $\beta_i$ into their parts $\lfloor \alpha_{ij} \rfloor$ and $\lfloor \beta_i \rfloor$ and fractional parts $\bar{\alpha}_{ij}, \bar{\beta}_i$.
$$
\begin{gathered}x_{B_i}+\sum_{j \notin B} \alpha_{i j} x_j=\beta_i \Leftrightarrow \\ x_{B_i}+\sum_{j \notin B}\left(\left\lfloor\alpha_{i j}\right\rfloor+\bar{\alpha}_{i j}\right) x_j=\left\lfloor\beta_i\right\rfloor+\bar{\beta}_i\end{gathered}
$$
Since all $x$ are non-negative, LHS becomes smaller if the fractional parts are discarded in the coefficients of $x$:
$$
x_{B_i}+\sum_{j \notin B}\left\lfloor\alpha_{i j}\right\rfloor x_j \leq\left\lfloor\beta_i\right\rfloor+\bar{\beta}_i
$$
The constructed inequality is now subtracted from the considered row equation giving an inequality which is valid for all integer solutions but not necessarily for non-integral solutions:
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
x_{B_i}+\sum_{j \notin B}\left(\left\lfloor\alpha_{i j}\right\rfloor+\bar{\alpha}_{i j}\right) x_j & =\left\lfloor\beta_i\right\rfloor+\bar{\beta}_i \\
-\left(x_{B_i}+\sum_{j \notin B}\left\lfloor\alpha_{i j}\right\rfloor x_j\right. & \left.\leq\left\lfloor\beta_i\right\rfloor\right) \\
\hline \sum_{j \notin B} \bar{\alpha}_{i j} x_j & \geq \bar{\beta}_i
\end{array}
$$
The final inequality is called a Gomory cut. 

## Cutting plane algorithm

$t=0, P^0=P$, fail $=$ false
**repeat**
    solve $\bar{z}^t=\max \left\{c x: x \in P^t\right\}$ ( $x^t$ is an opt solution)
    **if** $x^t \notin \mathbb{Z}^n$ **then**
        Solve the separation problem between $x^t$ and $F$
        (find inequality $\left(\pi^t, \pi_0^t\right) \in F$ so that $\pi^t x^t>\pi_0^t$ )
        **if** no inequality is found then **fail** $=$ true
        **else** $P^{t+1}=P^t \cap\left\{x: \pi^t x \leq \pi_0\right\}$
**until** $x^t \in \mathbb{Z}^n \vee$ fail = true
**if** fail $=$ false **then** $x^t$ optimal solution for IP
**else** use branch-and-bound to obtain IP
