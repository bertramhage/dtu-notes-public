### The Drivers of Environmental Impact (IPAT Equation)
The global environmental impact ($I$) is conceptually determined by three factors: **Population** ($P$), **Affluence** ($A$), and **Technology** ($T$).
- **The Challenge:** Since global population and affluence (consumption/GDP) are steadily increasing, the environmental impact will grow significantly unless the technology factor changes.
- **The Technology Gap:** To remain within safe "Planetary Boundaries," the technology factor ($T$)—defined as impact per created value—must become **4 to 20 times more efficient**.
- **Rebound Effect:** Improving technology alone is insufficient because increased efficiency often leads to increased consumption (the rebound effect); therefore, human consumption demands must also be addressed directly.

### Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) Framework
LCA is a comparative method used to assess environmental impacts across the entire life of a product, known as **"cradle to grave"**.
- **Holistic Scope:** It accounts for all stages: raw material extraction, manufacturing, distribution, use, and disposal. This prevents **"problem shifting,"** where an improvement in one stage simply moves the burden to another.
- **Course Scope (Tier 1 Assessment):** In this specific course, the assessment will focus on "Tier 1: Sustainability Footprint," which prioritizes practicality and global relevance. The specific indicators used are:
    - **LCA (Environmental):** Climate change (Carbon footprint). _Note: Eutrophication is added if agricultural products are involved_.
    - **LCC (Economic):** Production costs (including materials, labor, energy, transport, and maintenance).
    - **SLCA (Social):** Fair wages (comparing wages to minimum and non-poverty benchmarks).

### Defining the Object of Assessment: The Functional Unit
In LCA, you do not compare products directly; you compare the **service or function** they provide.
- **Quantitative Definition:** To ensure a fair comparison, the functional unit must be quantified by answering five specific questions: 
	- **What?** (Function)
	- **How much?** (Quantity)
	- **How well?** (Quality)
	- **How long?** (Duration)
	- **Where?** (Location).
- **Properties:** Defining this requires distinguishing between:
    - **Obligatory properties:** Essential for the product to function (e.g., a lamp _must_ provide light and have a CE label).
    - **Positioning properties:** Features that make it attractive (e.g., the design or color temperature of a lamp).
- **Practical Examples of Functional Units
	- *Example A: Aluminium Beer Can*:
	    - Context: The function is not just "a can," but the packaging and preservation of the liquid.
	    - Defined Functional Unit: "Packaging of **1000 l of beer** and keeping it fresh during transportation, retail and storage (**for up to 1 year**) in **Denmark**".
	- *Example B: Desktop Working Lamp*
	    - Context: The function is providing illumination for work, specifically replacing the need for ambient office light.
	    - Defined Functional Unit: "Providing **500 lumen light** to an office working place in average **4 hours per day**, **230 days per year** for **5 years** in **Denmark**".
