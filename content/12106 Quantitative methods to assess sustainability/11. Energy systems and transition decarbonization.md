### Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) Framework for Electricity Generation
To accurately assess sustainability, one must evaluate the total environmental impact of an electricity-producing unit over its entire life, not just during operation. This requires a Life Cycle Analysis (LCA) that accounts for raw material extraction, manufacturing, transport, operation (fuel consumption), and decommissioning.
- The Normalization Framework: To compare technologies, emissions are normalized per unit of energy produced. The specific formula for the total emission factor ($EF_j$) is defined as:
	$$EF_{j}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}\epsilon_{i,j}+m_{f}\epsilon_{f,j}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{r,i}\epsilon_{r,i,j}}{E}$$
    where $m_i$ is the mass of materials, $m_f$ is fuel consumption, $m_r$ is recycled mass (which can be subtracted), $\epsilon$ represents specific emission factors, and $E$ is the lifetime energy production.
- **Technology Disparity:** There is a massive divergence in Global Warming Potential (GWP) between technologies. Coal emits approximately 1000 g $CO_{2}e/kWh$, whereas low-carbon technologies like Wind, Nuclear, and Hydro emit between 5 and 25 g $CO_{2}e/kWh$.

### Materiality and Circularity in Renewable Energy
While renewable energy sources like wind do not consume fuel, they are not zero-emission due to the materials required for their construction.
- **Emissions Origin:** For wind turbines, 80-90% of carbon emissions stem from the material extraction and manufacturing phases, specifically steel for towers and foundations, and composites for blades. For example, manufacturing steel releases approximately 1.9 tons of $CO_2$ per ton of steel.
- **The Circularity Challenge:** Reducing the carbon footprint of renewables relies on circularity. Steel is highly recyclable, but turbine blades (glass fiber and epoxy) have historically been difficult to recycle. New methods, such as pyrolysis to separate fibers for remelting, are essential to closing this material loop and lowering lifecycle emissions.

### Planetary Boundaries and Scaling Scenarios
The ultimate goal of quantifying these emissions is to determine if the global electricity mix violates the planetary boundary set by the Paris Agreement ($1.5^{\circ}C$ warming limit).
- **The Violation Gap:** Current "Business as Usual" scenarios, assuming a doubling of electricity demand by 2050, are projected to violate the global warming potential boundary by 263%. Even the current mix of coal and gas alone will violate the $1.5^{\circ}C$ path by roughly 2045.
- **The Scaling Reality:** A critical insight from the modeling exercise is that simply copying a "green" developed nation's current strategy is insufficient. If the entire world adopted Denmark's 2022 electricity mix (high wind, bioenergy, but some fossil fuels) and doubled production, the planetary boundary would still be violated by 385%. This indicates that drastic changes in technology mix and low-carbon manufacturing are required beyond current transition rates.
