Brownian motion is a continuous time Markov process with a transition kernel that solves the diffusion equation
$$
\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}= \frac{1}{2} \frac{\partial^{2}p}{\partial y^{2}},
$$
where $p(y,t|x)$ is a probability density function, in $y$, of the stochastic variable $B(t_0+t)$, given $B(t_0)=x$.

We get
$$
p(y,t|x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2\pi t }}\exp\left( -\frac{1}{2t}(y-x)^2 \right)=\phi_t(y-x),
$$
where $\phi_t$ denotes the normal distribution with mean zero and variance $t$.

Thus, 
$$
Pr\left\{ B(t)≤y|B(0)=x \right\} =\Phi\left( \frac{y-x}{\sqrt{ t }} \right).
$$
>[!info] Definition
> >The definition of Brownian motion is very similar to the definition of the Poisson process.
>
>Brownian motion with diffusion coefficient $\sigma^{2}$ is a stochastic process $\left\{ B(t);t≥0 \right\}$ with the properties:
>
>(a) $B(t+s)-B(s)\sim N(0,\sigma^2t)$; $\sigma^{2}>0$ is a fixed parameter.
>
>(b) For every pair of disjoint time intervals $]t_1,t_2], ]t_2,t_3]$, with $0≤t_1<t_2≤t_3<t_4$ the increments $B(t_4)-B(t_3)$ and $B(t_2)-B(t_1)$ are independent random variables, and similarly for $n$ disjoint time intervals, where $n$ is an arbitrary positive integer.
>
>(c) $B(0)=0$, and $B(t)$ is continuous as a function of $t$.

The process $\tilde{B}(t)=B(t)/\sigma$ is the *standard Brownian motion* and has variance parameter 1.

## Properties

#### Covariance
The covariance of the Brownian motion 
$$
\mathrm{Cov}[B(s),B(t)]=\sigma^{2}\min(s,t),\quad \text{ for } s,t≥0.
$$
#### Invariance principle (central limit theorem)
The invariance principle for Brownian motion states that a properly rescaled random walk converges in distribution to a Brownian motion as the number of steps goes to infinity.

For a Brownian motion $B(t)$ and states $a,b$
$$
Pr\left\{ B(t)\text{ reaches }b \text{ before }a \right\}=\frac{a}{a+b},
$$
due to the invariance principle.
## Gaussian processes
A **multivariate Gaussian distribution** is an extension of the one-dimensional Gaussian distribution to multiple dimensions

$$
f(x)=\frac{(2\pi)^{-n/2}}{\det(\Sigma)}\exp\left( -\frac{1}{2}(x-\mu)^\top\Sigma ^{-1}(x-\mu) \right),
$$
where $x$ and $\mu$ are vectors, $\mathrm{dim}(x)=n$, and $\Sigma$ the covariance matrix.



For a random vector $X=(X_1,\dots,X_n), X_i\sim N(\mu_i,\sigma_i^{2})$
If for all $a\in\mathbb{R}^n$ then $aX$ is normal, then $X$ is multivariate Gaussian distributed.
$$
X\in N(\mu,\Gamma) \text{ if } \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_jX_j\in N(\alpha \mu,\alpha \Gamma \alpha')\quad \forall \alpha\in\mathbb{R}^n \, .
$$
Here
$$
\mu(t)=E[X(t)]
$$
and
$$
\Gamma(s,t)=E[\left\{ X(s)-\mu(s) \right\} \left\{ X(t)-\mu(t) \right\} ].
$$
## Reflection principle
Define $M(t)=\max_{0≤u≤t}B(u)$ then for a fixed $t≥u≥0$
$$
Pr\left\{ M(t)>x, B(t)>x \right\} = Pr\left\{ M(t)>x,B(t)<x \right\}
$$
$$
 \implies
Pr\left\{ M(t)>x \right\} =2\left( 1-\Phi\left( \frac{x}{\sqrt{ t }} \right) \right),
$$
where $x>0$.
Define $\tau_x=\min\left\{ t:B(t)=x \right\}$ then
$$
Pr\left\{ \tau_x≤t \right\} =Pr\left\{ M(t)>x \right\} =2\left( 1-\Phi\left( \frac{x}{\sqrt{ t }} \right) \right).
$$
