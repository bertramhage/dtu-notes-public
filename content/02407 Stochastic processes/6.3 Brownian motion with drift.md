sLet $\left\{ B(t),t≥0 \right\}$ be a standard Brownian motion process, and let $\mu$ and $\sigma>0$ be fixed. The Brownian motion with drift parameter $\mu$ and variance paramenter $\sigma^{2}$ is the process
$$
X(t)=\mu t+\sigma B(t) \text{ for } t≥0.
$$
## Absorption probability and mean time to absorption
Consider a gamblers ruin, similar to [1.2 Random walks](obsidian://open?vault=Noter&file=Stokastiske%20processer%2F1.2%20Random%20walks), such that for fixed $a<x$ and $b>x$: $T=T_{ab}=\min\left\{ t≥0,X(t)=a \text{ or }X(t)=b \right\}.$

Given $a<x$ and $b>x$, for a Brownian motion with drift parameter $\mu$ and variance parameter $\sigma^{2}$
$$
u(x)=Pr\left\{ X(T_{ab})=b|X(0)=x \right\}=\frac{e^{-2\mu x/\sigma^{2}}-e^{-2\mu a/\sigma^{2}}}{e^{-2\mu b/\sigma^{2}}-e^{-2\mu a/\sigma^{2}}}. 
$$
The mean time to exit an interval, for a Brownian motion with drift parameter $\mu$ and variance parameter $\sigma^{2}$ is
$$
E[T_{ab}|X(0)=x]=\frac{1}{\mu}(u(x)(b-a)-(x-a)).
$$

## Geometric Brownian motion
For a geometric Brownian motion with drift parameter $\alpha$ ${Z(t),t≥0}$
$$
X(t)=\log Z(t)
$$
is a Brownian motion with drift $\mu=\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}$ and variance parameter $\sigma^{2}$ and
$$
Z(t)=ze^{X(t)}=ze^{(\alpha-1/2 \sigma^{2})t+\sigma B(t)},
$$
is a geometric Brownian motion with $Z(0)=z$, where $B(t)$ is a standard Brownian motion with $B(0)=0$.

For a geometric Brownian motion if $t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n$ are time points then 
$$
\frac{Z(t_1)}{Z(t_0)}, \frac{Z(t_2)}{{Z(t_1)}},\dots, \frac{Z(t_n)}{Z(t_{n-1})}
$$
are independent random variables, so that the percentage changes over non-overlapping time intervals are independent.

For a geometric brownian motion $Z(t)$ with drift parameter $\alpha$
$$
E[Z(t)|Z(0)=z]=ze^{(\mu+1/2\sigma^{2})t}=ze^{\alpha t}
$$
and
$$
Var[Z(t)]=z^{2}e^{2\alpha t}(e^{\sigma^{2}t}-1).
$$
**Theorem 8.3**: Gamblers ruin
For a geometric Brownian motion with drift parameter $\alpha$ and variance parameter $\sigma^{2}$, and $A<1<B$
$$
Pr\left\{ \frac{Z(T)}{Z(0)}=B \right\} =\frac{1-A^{1-2\alpha/\sigma^{2}}}{B^{1-2\alpha/\sigma^{2}}-A^{1-2\alpha/\sigma^{2}}}.
$$
*Example*: Suppose that the fluctuations in the price of a share of stock in a certain company are well described by a geometric Brownian motion with drift  $\alpha=\frac{1}{10}$ and variance $\sigma^{2}=4$. A speculator buys a share of this stock at a price of $100 and will sell if ever the price rises to $110 (a profit) or drops to $95 (a loss). What is the probability that the speculator sells at a profit?
