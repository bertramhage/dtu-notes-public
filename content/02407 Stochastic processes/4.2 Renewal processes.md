A renewal (counting) process $\{N(t), t ≥ 0\}$ is a nonnegative integer-valued stochastic process that registers the successive occurrences of an event during the time interval $]0, t]$, where the times between consecutive events are. Thus, same as a Poisson process with the assumption of exponentiality relaxed.

Let $\{X_k\}_{k_=1}^\infty$ represent succesive durations between events, then
$$
W_n=X_1+X_2+\cdots X_n, n≥1,\quad (W_0=0),
$$
is called the *waiting time* and
$$
N(t)=\max\left\{ n≥0|W_n≤t \right\}
$$
the *counting process*.
![[Pasted image 20241123170525.png|500]]

Define 
$$
Pr\left\{ X_k≤t \right\} =F(t),\quad k=1,2,\dots,
$$
and
$$
E[N(t)]=M(t) \, ,
$$
the latter called the *renewal function*.

Then, considering $W_n=X_1+\cdots+X_n$,
$$
Pr\left\{ W_n≤t \right\} =F_n(t) = \int_{0}^{\infty} F_{n-1}(t-x) \, dF(x)=\int_{0}^{t} F_{n-1}(t-x) \, dF(x)
$$
and thus
$$
M(t)=E[N(t)]=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} Pr\left\{ W_k≤t \right\} =\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} F_k(t) \,.  \, 
$$

Using equations $(7.6)$ and $(7.8)$ it can be derived that the *mean arrival time of next event* is
$$
E[W_{N(t)+1}]=E[X_1]E\left[ \sum_{j=2}^{N(t)+1} X_j \,  \right]=\mu(M(t)+1),
$$
with $\mu=E[X_1]$.

## The Asymptotic Behavior of Renewal Processes
A large number of the functionals that have explicit expressions for Poisson renewal processes are far more difficult to compute for other renewal processes. There are, however, many simple formulas that describe the asymptotic behavior, for large values of $t$, of a general renewal process.

>[!info] The Elementary Renewal Theorem
>For all renewal functions we assert that they are asymptotically linear, in the sense that
>$$
>\lim_{ t \to \infty } \frac{M(t)}{t}=\lim_{ t \to \infty } \frac{E[N(t)]}{t}=\frac{1}{\mu},
>$$
>where $\mu=E[X_k]$ is the mean interoccurrence time.

Using the renewal theorem we can derive the following.

#### Renewal density for continuous $F(x)$
For continuous lifetimes $X_1, X_2, \dots$ with pdf $f(x)$ the renewal function is differentiable and
$$
m(t)=\frac{\mathrm{d}M(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f_n(t), \, 
$$
where $f_n(t)$ is the probability density function for $W_n=X_1+\cdots+X_n$.
Here 
$$
\lim_{ t \to \infty } m(t)=\lim_{ t \to \infty } \frac{\mathrm{d}M(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{1}{\mu},
$$
and for lifetimes with finite mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$
$$
\lim_{ t \to \infty } \left( M(t)-\frac{t}{\mu} \right)=\frac{\sigma^2-\mu^2}{2\mu^2},\quad Var(X_k)=\sigma^2.
$$

#### Asymptotic distribution
For large values of $t$ the number of renewals $N(t)$ is approximately normally distributed with mean and variance
$$
\lim_{ t \to x } \frac{E[N(t)]}{t}=\frac{1}{\mu},\quad \lim_{ t \to \infty } \frac{Var[N(t)]}{t}=\frac{\sigma^2}{\mu^3},
$$
as it converges to
$$
\lim _{t \rightarrow \infty} \operatorname{Pr}\left\{\frac{N(t)-t / \mu}{\sqrt{t \sigma^2 / \mu^3}} \leq x\right\}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \pi}} \int_{-\infty}^x \mathrm{e}^{-y^2 / 2} \mathrm{~d} y .
$$

#### Excess, age and total life
Define *excess, age* and *total life*:
$$
\gamma_t=W_{N(t)+1}-t,\quad \delta_t=t-W_{N(t)},\quad \beta_t=\gamma_t+\delta_t = W_{N(t)+1}-W_{N(t)}.
$$
![[Pasted image 20241124122605.png|500]]
Then
$$
\lim_{ t \to \infty } Pr\left\{ \gamma_t≤x \right\} =\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{0}^{x} (1-F(u)) \, du, 
$$
$$
\lim_{ t \to \infty } Pr\left\{ \gamma_t≥x,\delta_t≥y \right\} =\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{x+y}^{\infty} (1-F(z)) \, dz,
$$
and
$$
\lim_{ t \to \infty } Pr\left\{ \beta_t≤x \right\} =\frac{\int_{0}^{x} t \, dF(t) }{\mu}.
$$
