Grouped into three different types
## Preprocessing
- Mitigating group imbalance via more data, upsampling or augmentation
- Within-group standardisation or harmonisation
- If possible: standardise data quality across groups

## In-training mitigation
Constrain the training of the algorithm to only obtain fair classifiers
Eg:
- Adversarial training to avoid encoding of sensitive attributes
- Adding terms to the loss function that encourage a particular fairness criterion
- Using causal modelling to eliminate unwanted causal dependencies in model

## Post-hoc mitigation
Message a biased model to make it less biased

### Randomisation to achieve equalised odds
Randomisation interpolates TPR/FPR by *sampling* pairs of thresholds according to an interpolant probability $p$

## 1.3 Pitfalls in applying algorithmic fairness

- Label bias is frequent, hard to discover, and hard to quantify – commonly true labels cannot be observed
	- Unable to measure algorithmic fairness
	- Unable to mitigate algorithmic fairness
	- At risk of creating new algorithmic bias if we try

- Marginal invariance leads to the independence fairness criterion – equal acceptance rates
- Class-conditional invariance leads to an extra strict version of equalized odds – a very harsh constraint, as all properties that relate even vaguely to group membership will be aligned
