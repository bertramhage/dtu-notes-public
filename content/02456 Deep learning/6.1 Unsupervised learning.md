## Unsupervised learning

Data is just $\mathbf{x}$, not input output pair $(\mathbf{x}, y)$

**Goal**: Build a model $p_\theta(\mathbf{x})$ of the data generating distribution $p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})$

### Latent variables
Many unsupervised learning approaches use **latent variables $\mathbf{z}$**
- Discrete $z$: k-means, mixture models,...
- Continuous $z$: PCA, ICA, NMF, state space models...

## Autoencoders
Models such as the ones above can be thought of autoencoders:
- **Encoder**: function: $f:\mathbf{x}\to \mathbf{z}$
- **Decoder**: function: $g: \mathbf{z}\to \mathbf{x}$
- **Reconstruction**: $\hat{\mathbf{x}}=g(f(\mathbf{x}))$
- We want $\hat{\mathbf{x}}\approx \mathbf{x}$

Regularisation avoids learning the identity function:
- Bottleneck autoencoder: limit the dimensionality of $\mathbf{z}$
- Sparse autoencoder: penalize activations of $\mathbf{z}$
- Denoising autoencoder: inject noise to input $\mathbf{x}$
- ...

### Bottleneck autoencoder

Loss is
$$
L(\phi_e,\phi_d)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim p_\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})\left[ \mid\mid \mathbf{x}-g_{\phi_{d}}(\mathbf{x})\mid\mid^{2} \right] 
$$
where $f$ and $g$ are neural nets.

### Latent variable models

**Generative process**
$$
\mathbf{z}\sim p(\mathbf{z})
$$
$$
\mathbf{x}\sim p(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z}) = \Phi(\mathbf{x}|f_\theta(\mathbf{z}))
$$
where 
- $\mathbf{z}\in \mathcal{Z}^M$ is the continuous latent variable
- $\mathbf{x}\in \mathcal{X}$ is the observed variable
- the function $f_\theta:\mathcal{Z}^M\to H$ (deep) neural network called the decoder
- $\left\{ \Phi(\cdot|\eta) \right\}_{\eta \in H}$ is a parametric family called the output density.

We only observe $\mathbf{x}$, and the marginal density of $\mathbf{x}$ is
$$
p(\mathbf{x})=\int p(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})p(\mathbf{z}) \, d\mathbf{z} 
$$
Posterior distribution (probabilistic encoding):
$$
p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})=\frac{p(\mathbf{x})|\mathbf{z}p(\mathbf{z})}{p(\mathbf{x})}
$$

### Amortised variational inference (VI)
We want to learn the parameters $\theta$ of the decoder.

The log-likelihood objective is
$$
\ell(\theta)=\sum_{n=1}^{N} \log p(\mathbf{x}_n) \text{ where } p(\mathbf{x}_n) = \int p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_n | \mathbf{z})p(\mathbf{z}) \, d\mathbf{z}  \, 
$$
However, $p(\mathbf{x}_n)$ is intractable for non-linear decoders.

**ELBO**: VI approximatively maximises the log-likelihood, $\ell(\theta)$, by maximising the ELBO:
$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}(\theta,\phi)&=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}\sim q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})}\left[ \log \frac{p_\theta(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})p(\mathbf{z})}{q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})} \right] \\
&=\underbrace{ \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}\sim q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})}[\log p_\phi(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})] }_{ \text{reconstruction term} }-\underbrace{ KL[q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})\mid\mid p(\mathbf{z})] }_{ \text{regularisation term} }\le \ell(\theta)
\end{align}
$$

We use the variational posterior
$$
q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}) = \Psi(\mathbf{z}|g_\phi(\mathbf{x}))
$$
where
- $\left\{ \Psi(\cdot|\mathcal{K}) \right\}_{\mathcal{K}\in K}$ is the variational family over $\mathcal{Z}^M$ (for $\mathbb{R}^M$ usually Gaussian)
- $g_\phi:\mathcal{X}\to K$ is a neural net called the encoder or inference network
- $g_\phi$ transforms a datapoint $\mathbf{x}$ into parameters of the variational distribution

### Generative adversarial network (GANs)

A GAN consists of:
- A **generator** that learns to generate plausible data
- A **discriminator** that learns to distinguish between fake (generated) and real data

![[Pasted image 20251020135821.png|400]]

The model is learned through a minimax game:
$$
\min_G \max_D \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim p_\text{data}(\mathbf{x})}[\log D(\mathbf{x})]+\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}\sim p(\mathbf{z})}[1-D(\mathbf{z})]
$$
where the discriminator $D(x)$ returns the probability that $x$ is real and the generator $G(\mathbf{z})$ transforms noise $\mathbf{z}$ to an image $\mathbf{x}$.

## Measuring performance

- We can compare test-set log-likelihood
- For GANs:
	- Inception score: Average distance between generated images class and test-set images classes
		$IS=\exp(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim p_\text{test}(\mathbf{x})}[KL[p(y|\mathbf{x})\mid\mid p_{\text{test}}(y)]])$
	- Fréchet inception distance: Wasserstein-2 distance between two Gaussians fitted to the last pooling-layer an Inception-v3 network
