We want to find $\hat{\phi}=\arg \min_\phi L(\phi)$
- Initialize $\phi ^{(0)}$ randomly
- Iterate (for $t\in1,\dots,k$)
	- Step 1 (gradient): Iterated $\nabla_\phi L(\phi ^{(t)})=\begin{pmatrix} \frac{\partial L}{\partial \phi_1}\\\dots\\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial \phi_D}\end{pmatrix}$
	- Step 2 (update parameters): $\phi ^{(t+1)}-\eta \nabla_\phi L(\phi ^{(t)})$
Where $k$ is the number of iterations, and $\eta$ is the step-size or learning rate

> Gradient is 0 at local minima and saddle points $\implies$ can lead to GD becoming stuck before global optimum.

## Stochastic gradient descent
At each step, the gradient is calculated on a minibatch
$$
\phi ^{(t+1)}=\phi ^{(t)}-\sum_{i\in \mathcal{B}_t} \frac{\partial l_i(\phi ^{(t)})}{\partial \phi}
$$
The batch $\mathcal{B}_t \subseteq \left\{ 1,\dots,n \right\}$ index-set is drawn stochasticcally (usually w/o replacement, and a full poss of the data is called an *epoch*).

$l_i(\phi ^{(t)})$ is the loss on $(x_i,y_i)$ assuming that $L(\phi)=\sum_{i=1}^n l_i(\phi)$

## Momentum
Use a weighted (decaying) average of previous gradients
- $m^{(t+1)}=\beta m^{(t)}+(1-\beta)\sum_{i\in \mathcal{B}_t} \frac{\partial l_i(\phi ^{(t)})}{\partial \phi}$
- $\phi ^{(t+1)}=\phi ^{(t)}-\eta m^{(t+1)}$
where $\beta\in[0,1[$ controls the smoothing.

## Adam (Adaptive moment estimation)
In Adam, we normalise the gradients by their variance

We estimate the first and second moments (weighted) of the gradients
- $m^{(t+1)}=\beta m^{(t)}+(1-\beta)\nabla_\phi L(\phi ^{(t)})$
- $v^{(t+1)}=\gamma v^{(t)}+(1-\gamma)(\nabla_\phi L(\phi ^{(t)}))^2$

Compensate for initial values close to zero
$$
\tilde{m}^{(t+1)}= \frac{m^{(t+1)}}{1-\beta ^{t+1}} \text{ and } \tilde{v^{(t+1)}}=\frac{v^{(t)}}{1-\gamma ^{t+1}}
$$
Update parameters:
$$
\phi ^{(t+1)}=\phi ^{(t)}-\eta \frac{\tilde{m}^{(t+1)}}{\sqrt{ \tilde{v}^{(t+1)} }+\epsilon}.
$$
## Backpropagation (Computing derivatives)
Consider a scalar only architecture

$$
\begin{aligned}
& h^{(1)}=\sigma_1\left(w_1 x\right) \\
& h^{(2)}=\sigma_2\left(w_2 x\right) \\
& y=w_3 h^{(2)}
\end{aligned}
$$

and some loss function

Using the chain rule, calculate derivative in a backward pass

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_3}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial y} \frac{\partial y}{\partial w_3} \\
& \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_2}=\frac{\partial L \partial y}{\partial y \partial h^{(2)}} \frac{\partial h^{(2)}}{\partial w_2} \\
& \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_1}=\frac{\partial L \partial y}{\partial y \partial h^{(2)}} \frac{\partial h^{(2)}}{\partial h^{(1)}} \frac{\partial h^{(1)}}{\partial w_1}
\end{aligned}
$$
## Parameter initialisation
We random initialize the weights eg $\phi_i=\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})$
- If $\sigma^{2}$ is too *small* the signal vanishes as it passes through the network
- If $\sigma^{2}$ is too *big* the signal grows as it passes through the network

A reasonable criterion for keeping the information flow for all $i, i'$
$$
Var[h_i^{(l)}]=Var[h_{i'}^{(l-1)}]
$$
For ReLU activation, this implies that
$$
\sigma^{2}=\frac{2}{D}
$$
where $D$ is the dimension of the layer.
