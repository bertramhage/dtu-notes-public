Dynamic programming can be used to solve optimisation problems, when
1. Problems can be split into subproblems
2. Overlapping subproblems

Eg. with Fibonacci numbers:

![[Pasted image 20250923152745.png|400]]


>[!info] Dynamic programming components
>- Recursion (split problem into smaller problems)
>	- Eg. $n!=n\cdot(n-1)\cdots 3\cdot2\cdot1$
>	- Well suited problems: Tree, DAG, etc.
>- Start condition
>	- Eg. $f(1)=x$
>- Evaluate from start condition, save values in table

## Steps

>[!info] Dynamical programming steps
>- **State**: Define the subproblem structure
>- **Initialisation**: Calculate the optimal solutions to trivial states
>- **Transition**: Recursively define the value of a state as a function of previously calculated states
>- **Principle of Optimality**: The value of a state should represent the optimal solution to the subproblem – the final state should represent the original problem
>- **Optimal solution**: Retrace your decisions to construct the optimal solution

## Examples of dynamic programming problems
### Shortest path problem
Consider a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG)
![[Pasted image 20250923153428.png|500]]
Shortest path from $s$ to $t$ passes $p$ $\implies$ sub-paths $(s,p)$ and $(p,t)$ are shortest path from $s\to p$ and $p\to t$ respectively.

>[!example] **Formulation**:
>
>$d(j)$ denote the length of a shortest path from $s$ to $j$. Then
>$$
>d(v)=\min_{i\in V^-(v)}\left\{ d(i)+c_{iv} \right\} 
>$$
>where $V^-(v)$ denotes predecessor nodes of $v$.
>$d(s)=0$

**Running time** is $O(V+E)$.

### The 0-1 knapsack problem

$$
\begin{array}{ll}\max & \sum_{j=1}^n c_j x_j \\ \text { s.t. } & \sum_{j=1}^n a_j x_j \leq b \\ & x \in\{0,1\}^n\end{array}
$$
where $c_j, a_j, \text{ and } b$ are positive integers.

Consider a related problem $P_r(\lambda)$:
$$
\begin{array}
ff_r(\lambda) =& \max & \sum_{j=1}^{r} c_j x_j \\
& s.t. & \sum_{j=1}^{r} a_jx_j\le \lambda \\
& & x \in \left\{ 0,1 \right\}^r \,  \, 
\end{array}
$$
where $\lambda \in \left\{ 1,\dots,b \right\}$.

Thus we get 
$$
f_r(\lambda)=\max\begin{cases}
f_{r-1}(\lambda) \\
c_r+f_{r-1}(\lambda-a_r)
\end{cases}
$$
$f_0(\lambda)\text{ for }\lambda=0,\dots,b$.

Optimal solution to full problem is found as $f_n(b)$.

**Running time** is $O(nb)$

### Uncapacitated lot-sizing (ULS)
Decide on a production plan for an $n$-period horizon for a single product
- Time horizon $t \in \left\{ 0,1,\dots,n \right\}$
- $f_t$: fixed cost of producing in period $t$
- $p_t$: unit production cost in period $t$
- $h_t$: unit storage cost in period $t$
- $d_t$: demand in period $t$

**ULS as a network problem**
![[Pasted image 20250923162530.png|400]]

>[!example] MIP Formulation
>$$
>\begin{array}{lll}\min & \sum_{t=1}^n h s_t+f y_t & \\ \text { s.t. } & s_{t-1}+x_t=d_t+s_t, & t=1, \ldots, n \\ & x_t \leq M y_t, & t=1, \ldots, n \\ & y_t \in\{0,1\}, x_t, s_t \geq 0 & t=1, \ldots, n\end{array}
>$$
>where $y_t=1$ if produce in period $t$
>$$
>x_t>0\implies y_t=1
>$$

**Recursion**
Let $H(k)$ be the cheapest production cost for periods $1,2,\dots,k$ where the period starts with empty stock
$$
H(k)=\min_{1\le t\le k}\left\{ H(t-1)+f+hv_{tk} \right\} 
$$
$H(0)=0$,
where $v_{ij}$ is the number of "item storage-months" from month $i$ to month $j$ (where we produce in month $i$ everything that is needed up to month $j$):
$$
v_{i j}=\sum_{\ell=i}^j d_{\ell}(\ell-i).
$$

Optimal solution is found at $H(n)$.

>[!example]- Example
>![[Pasted image 20250923164311.png|300]]
>![[Pasted image 20250923164237.png|500]]
>

**If we have variable production costs $p_i$**
Let $w_{ij}$ denote production cost if produce in month $i$ all demand up to month $j$
$$
w_{ij}=p_i \sum_{\ell=i}^{j}  \, d_{\ell}
$$
Recursion:
$$
H(k)=\min_{1\le t\le k}\left\{ H(t-1)+f+w_{tk}+hv_{tk} \right\} 
$$

**If we have variable holding costs $h_i$**
Let $c_{ij}$ denote storage costs for all demand from month $i$ to month $j$
$$
c_{ij}=\sum_{\ell=i}^{j} d_{\ell}\sum_{\mu=i}^{\ell-1} h_{\mu} \,  \, 
$$
Recursion:
$$
H(k)=\min_{1\le t\le k}\left\{ H(t-1)+f+w_{tk}+c_{tk} \right\} 
$$
