Linear models considered so far took the form
$$
y=f(b+w_1x_1+\cdots+w_Dx_D)=f\left( b+\sum_{i=1}^{D} w_ix_i \,  \right)
$$
where $f$ is a non-linear function and $\left\{ w_i \right\}$ are weights and $b$ is a bias term. Eg. $f$ is a sigmoid for logistic regression or identity for linear regression.

![[Pasted image 20251020082852.png|200]]
With linear combinations of $M$ fixed basis functions $\phi_m(\mathbf{x})$:
$$
y=f\left( b+\sum_{m=1}^{M} w_m\phi_m(\mathbf{x}) \,  \right),
$$
eg.
$$
y=f\left( b+\sum_{m=1}^{M} w_mg\left( b_m+\sum_{i=1}^{D} w_{m,i}x_i \,  \right) \,  \right),
$$
where $g$ is a non-linearity.

Alternative perspective
$$
h_m=g\left( b_m+\sum_{i=1}^{D} w_{m,i}x_i \,  \right)
$$
$$
y=f\left( b+\sum_{m=1}^{M} w_mh_m \,  \right).
$$
## Activation functions

**Sigmoid function**:
$$
\sigma(x)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-x}}\in]0,1[
$$
**Tanh**:
$$
\tanh(x)\in]-1,1[
$$

**ReLU**:
$$
\max(0,x)
$$

## Predictions tasks

### Regression
Target variables $y\in\mathbb{R}$
$$
\hat{y}=g(b+\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{h}^{(L)})=b+\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{h}^{(L)}
$$
where $\mathbf{h}^{(L)}$ is the output of the last hidden layer.

Loss function (MSE):
$$
L=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N} (y_j-\hat{y}_j)^{2} \, .
$$

### Binary classification
Target variables $y\in\left\{ 0,1 \right\}$
$$
\hat{y}=p(y=1)=\text{sigmoid}(b+\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{h}^{(L)}).
$$
Decision predict class 1 if $\hat{y}>\tau$, else class 0, where $\tau \in[0,1]$.

Loss function (binary cross-entropy):
$$
L=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N} y_j\log(\hat{y}_j)+(1-y_j)\log(1-\hat{y}_j) .\,
$$

### Multi-class classification
Target variables $y\in\left\{ 1,\dots ,C\right\}$ one-hot encoded
$$
\hat{\mathbf{y}}=\text{softmax}(\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{Wh}^{(L)})
$$
$$
\hat{y}_c=p(y=c)=\frac{\exp(b_c+\mathbf{w}_c^\top\mathbf{h}^{(L)})}{\sum_{k=1}^{C}\exp(b_k+\mathbf{w}_k^\top\mathbf{h}^{(L)})  \, }.
$$
Decision: Predict class $c=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_c(\hat{y}_c)$.

Loss function (multi-class cross-entropy):
$$
L=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N} \sum_{c=1}^{C}y_{j,c}\log(\hat{y}_{j,c})  \, . \, 
$$
