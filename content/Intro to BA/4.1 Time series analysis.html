Components of a time-series

  • Trend: A long-term upwards or downward movement in the data, indication a general increase or decrease over time
  • Seasonality: A repeating pattern in the data that occurs at regular intervals, such as daily, weekly, monthly, or yearly.
  • Cyclical: A pattern in the data that repeats itself after a specific number of observations (unspecified time interval), which is not necessarily related to seasonality
  • Irregular fluctuations: Random fluctuations in the data that cannot easily be explained by trend, seasonality, or cycle.

Time-series decomposition

Additive decomposition When variation is constant yt = St + Tt + Rt Multiplicative decomposition When variation changes with time yt = St × Tt × Rt ![[Pasted image 20251020091319.png|400]] ## Stationarity A stationary time series is one whose statistical properties are constant over time - Strong stationarity: Joint probability distribution of a sub-sequence of variables does not change over time; i.e., joint distribution of yt, yt + 1, …, yt + n is exactly the same as the joint distribution of yt + k, yt + k + 1, …, yt + k + n. - Weak stationarity: Same mean and variance at all time points, and the covariance between variables at any two time points t and t − k (autocovariance) depends only on k and not the location of points in time

Handling non-stationarity

Trend adjustment: Eliminate the trend

  1. Fit a regression model describing the trend component and then subtract it out of the original observations E(yt) = β0 + β1t xt = yt − E(yt)
  2. Differencing: simpler and does not assume a fixed trend throughout the time series xt = yt − yt − 1
  3. Transformations to stabilize variance (eg log. power transformations)

Seasonality 1. Seasonal adjustment: In the simple additive decomposition model, adjust each observation by how much the season mean tends to be below or above the global (stationary) mean yt − St 2. Seasonal differencing: If m is the seasonality, yt − yt − m is the seasonally differenced data

Autocorrelation

Generally, autocorrelation is the correlation of a signal yt with a lagged copy of itself yt − L.

[!info]- Partial correlation Recall correlation between two random variables X and Y $$ Corr(X,Y)=\rho_{XY}=\frac{COV(X,Y)}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y} $$

For three random variables X, Y, Z consider simple linear regressions X̂ = a1 + b1Z,  Ŷ = a2 + b2Z and errors X* = X − X̂,  Y* = Y − Ŷ Partial correlation between X and Y after adjusting for Z is defined as the correlation between X* and Y*, ρX*Y*

Partial autocorrelation where the effect of intermediate lags is removed. Thus, partial autocorrelation with lag L = h is given by: pacf(L = h) = Corr(yt + h − ŷt + h, yt − ŷt)
