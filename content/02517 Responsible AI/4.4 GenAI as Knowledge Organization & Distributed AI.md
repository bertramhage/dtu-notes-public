### 1. GenAI as a Knowledge Organization System (KOS)

We can analyze Large Language Models (LLMs) not just as tools, but as systems that organize and retrieve knowledge.

- **The Problem of Fragmentation:** Unlike traditional libraries that use **Controlled Vocabularies** (precise, distinct definitions), LLMs rely on **Natural Language**, which mixes different "voices" and divergent views. This leads to fragmented knowledge where the theoretical criteria for selecting and interpreting sources are hidden from the user.
    
- **Representation Limits:** LLMs and image models often struggle with higher-order conceptualization, such as **subject-object ordering**. For example, models based on statistical correlation may fail to distinguish between "a horse riding an astronaut" and "an astronaut riding a horse" if the model relies purely on co-occurrence rather than structural logic.
    
- **Probability vs. Reality:** Models approximate likelihood (e.g., mean-field approximation), which can result in "unlikely" or impossible events (like inanimate objects performing human actions) being assigned fake high probabilities.
    

### 2. Mitigating Structure Issues

- **Upper Ontologies:** One solution is applying an "upper ontology" (explicit rules or hierarchies) to filter or guide the model's output. However, this is difficult to maintain and can lead to conflicting definitions or censorship.
    
- **Technological Essence:** The negative effects of these technologies often stem not from the tech itself, but from the tendency to benefit a minority in power (centralization).
    

### 3. Distributed and Decentralized AI

Moving away from centralized "temples" of knowledge, modern approaches focus on distributed systems:

- **Federated Learning:** A distributed approach where training happens locally on devices (preserving privacy/energy) and updates are sent to a central server to consolidate the global model.
    
- **Decentralized Agents:** Networks of nodes (Local SLMs) that communicate directly without a central provider to distribute tasks and aggregate responses.
    
- **Test-Time Scaling:** A method to improve Small Language Model (SLM) quality by augmenting inputs (transforming text/images) and aggregating probabilities from multiple parallel devices. This allows smaller models to achieve better accuracy and speed through parallel processing.
