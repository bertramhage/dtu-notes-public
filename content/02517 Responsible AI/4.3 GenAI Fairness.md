 The sources of bias in generative AI are likely similar as for predictive AI – **but** there are so many more ways for bias to take effect, harder to measure

We might seek to generalise the algorithmic fairness definition from [[1.1 Fairness]]
- *Independence*: Equal acceptance rates across groups
- *Separation*: Equal TPR, FPR, TNR, FNR across groups
- *Sufficiency*: Equal positive/negative predictive value: Given prediction, equal probability that it is true

### Obtaining fair(er) LLM decisions for classification-like prompts

- **Scoring reasoning steps in downstream inference**: At inference time, we draw $n_{\text{test}}$ chains $\{\mathbf{z}_k(x)\}^{n_{\text{test}}}_{k=1}$ for the new prompt $x$. Each step receives a fairness core $f_{\theta}(z_{_k,t})$, and the chain-level score is the mean
$$
r_k(x)=\frac{1}{T_k}\sum_{t=1}^{T_k} \sigma(f_\theta(z_{k,t}(x))) \, .
$$
- Scoring incurs $O(n_{\text{test}}T_{\max})$ calls to $f_{\theta}$, negligible compared to LLM generation with CoT prompting. We note that our goal is not to terminate or edit a chain when an unfair step is detected. All reasoning is preserved for accuracy and auditability of the final decision.
- **Aggregating final answer**: To aggregate the final answer over the $n_{\text{test}}$ reasoning chains, we convert the chain-level scores into weights
$$
w_k(x)=\frac{\exp(r_k(x)/\tau)}{}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\text{test}}} \exp(r_j(x)/\tau)\quad , \tau>0, \, 
$$
	and compute the final answer $\hat{a}(x)$ bt a weighted vite iver the $n_{\text{test}}$ candidate answers $\{a_k(x)\}^{n_{\text{test}}}_{k_=1}$ emitted at the ends of the chains:
$$
\hat{a}(x)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_a \sum_{k:a_k(x)=a} w_k(x).  \, 
$$
