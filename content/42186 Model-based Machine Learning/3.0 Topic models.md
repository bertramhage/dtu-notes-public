Predict
$$
\hat{y}=f(\mathbf{x}_\text{text}, \mathbf{x}_L),
$$
where $\mathbf{x}_\text{text}$ are variables coming from text data, and $\mathbf{x}_L$ are non-textual variables.

## Bag-of-Words (BoW) representation

- Replace words by stems
- Remove stopwords
- The dictionary is a collection of $C$ words (stems)
- Calculate frequency of all words in $C$

## Topic modelling in statistical models

>[!info] Topic Modelling
>Each document describes a set of $K$ concepts (or topics), where $K\ll C$ ($C$ is vocabulary size).
>Each topic is *latent*.

Let
$$
\mathbf{x}_{\text{text}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}_{\text{topic 1}} \\
\mathbf{x}_\text{topic 2} \\
\dots \\
\mathbf{x}_{\text{topic K}}\end{bmatrix}
$$
quantify how much each topic $k$ exists in a document.

This is a dimensionality reduction from BoW $C\to K$.

### Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA)
Generate a set of $I$ documents ($i=1,\dots,I$), each one being a sequence of words $\mathbf{w}_i$:
1. For each topic $k\in\{ 1,\dots,K \}$, there is a vector $\phi_k$ of $C$ words such that $p(\phi_k|\beta)=\text{Dirichlet}(\beta)$
2. For each document $i$:
	1. There is a vector of $K$ topics, $\theta_i$, such that $p(\theta|\alpha)=\text{Dirichlet}(\mathbf{\alpha})$
	2. For each of the words, $j\in\{ 1,\dots,\mid\mid \mathbf{w}_i\mid\mid \}$, we have
		1. A topic assignment, $z_{ij}(z_{i,j}\in\{ (1,\dots,K) \})$ such that $p(z_{i,j}|\theta_i)=\text{Categorical}(\theta_i)$
		2. A word, $w_{ij}$, such that $p(w_{i,j}|\phi_{z_{i,j}})=\text{Categorical}(\phi_{z_{i,j}})$

**Joint distribution**
$$p(\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{Z}, \mathbf{\Theta}, \mathbf{\Phi} | \alpha, \beta) = \left( \prod_{i=1}^{I} p(\theta_i | \alpha) \prod_{j=1}^{||\mathbf{w}i||} p(w{i,j} | \phi_{z_{i,j}}) , p(z_{i,j} | \theta_i) \right) \prod_{k=1}^{K} p(\phi_k | \beta)$$
Common implementations: Gibbs sampling or variational inference.
