For an intractable distribution $p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})$ consider a family of tractable distributions $q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{\nu})$ – the variational distribution.

Find the variational parameters $\mathbf{\nu}$ that makes $q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{\nu})$ as close as possible to the true posterior $p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})$.

## Mean-field variational inference
To obtain a tractable family of distributions for $q(\mathbf{z})$ we relax some constraints in the true distributions (eg. dependencies).

For example we can assume a fully factorized approximation

![[Pasted image 20260502112249.png|500]]

## Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence
The KL-divergence is defined as
$$
\mathbb{KL}(q(\mathbf{z})\mid\mid p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}))=\int_z q(\mathbf{z})\log \frac{q(\mathbf{z})}{p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})}=\mathbb{E}_q\left[ \log \frac{q(\mathbf{z})}{p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})} \right]
$$
Notice
$$
\mathbb{KL}(q(\mathbf{z})\mid\mid p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})) \neq \mathbb{KL}(p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})\mid\mid q(\mathbf{z}))
$$
## KL minimization
As $p(\mathbf{z}\mid \mathbf{x})$ is still intractable we use the evidence lower bound (ELBO) to minimize the KL-divergence
$$
\mathbb{KL}(q(\mathbf{z})\mid\mid p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}))=-\underbrace{ (\mathbb{E}_q[\log p(\mathbf{z,x})]-\mathbb{E}_q[\log(q(\mathbf{z}))]) }_{ \text{ELBO }\mathcal{L}(q) }+\log(p(\mathbf{x}))
$$
