>[!info] Probability interpretations
>“The probability that a coin will land heads is 0.5”
>
>**Frequentist interpretation**:
>Probabilities represent long run frequencies of events - e.g. if we flip the coin many times, we expect it to land heads about half the times
>
>**Bayesian interpretation**:
>Probability is used to quantify our *uncertainty* about something. It is related to *information* rather than repeated trials – eg. we believe the coin is equally likely to land heads or tails on the next toss.

### Frequentist vs Bayesian in practice
Consider that you have a probabilistic model with parameters $\theta$. Given that you observe some data $X$, you want to estimate $\theta$.

A frequentist approach would be to use MLE:
$$
\mathbf{\theta}_{MLE}=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_\theta(\log p(\mathbf{X|\theta}))
$$
Taking a step towards a Bayesian approach, consider a prior $p(\mathbf{\theta})$ and use a **maximum-a-posteriori** (MAP) estimation:
$$
\mathbf{\theta}_{MAP}=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_\mathbf{\theta}(\log p(\mathbf{X|\theta})+\log(p(\mathbf{\theta})))
$$
Both MLE and MAP provide point-estimates of $\mathbf{\theta}$. In a fully Bayesian approach we perform Bayesian inference of the posterior distribution over $\mathbf{\theta}$:
$$
p(\mathbf{\theta|X})= \frac{p(\mathbf{X|\theta})p(\mathbf{\theta})}{p(\mathbf{X})}.
$$
