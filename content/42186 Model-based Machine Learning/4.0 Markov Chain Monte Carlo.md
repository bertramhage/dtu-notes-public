Goal: Approximate posterior distribution of latent variables $\mathbf{z}$ given observations $\mathbf{x}$: $p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})$.

This is often *intractable* due to high dimensionality of latent space and complex form of posterior distribution.

## Monte Carlo methods
Obtain a set of *samples* $\mathbf{z}^{(s)}$, for $s \in\{ 1,\dots,S \}$, drawn independently from the distribution $p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})$.
Allows to approximate expectations as finite sums:
$$
\mathbf{E}_{p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})}[f(\mathbf{z})]\approx \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^Sf(\mathbf{z}^{(s)})
$$
Let $\mathcal{D}=\{ \mathbf{x}_n,y_n \}^N_{n=1}$ denote our dataset of observed variables
$$
\mathbf{E}_{p(\mathbf{z}|\mathcal{D})}[p(y_*|\mathbf{x}_*, \mathbf{z})]=\int p(y_*|\mathbf{x}_*,\mathbf{z})p(\mathbf{z}|\mathcal{D}) \, dz\approx \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}p(y_*|\mathbf{x}_*,\mathbf{z}^{(s)}),  \,  
$$
where $\mathbf{z}^{(s)}\sim p(\mathbf{z}|\mathcal{D})$.

> Requires us to sample from $p(\mathbf{z}|\mathcal{D})$ still!

### Properties of Monte Carlo
- Estimator is unbiased
- Variance shrinks proportionally to $\frac{1}{S}$:
$$
\mathbf{V}_{p(\{ \mathbf{z}^{(s)} \}|\mathbf{x})}[\hat{f}]=\frac{1}{S^{2}}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathbf{V}_{p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})}[f(\mathbf{z})]= \frac{1}{S}\mathbf{V}_{p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})}[f(\mathbf{z})]  \, ,
$$
where $\hat{f}=\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S} f(\mathbf{z}^{(s)}),\quad p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}) \,$.

### Sampling from posterior distributions
Assume we know CDF of $p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})$.

- Draw mass to the left of point $u\sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)$
- Use inverse CDF to obtain sample $y(u)=h^{-1}(u)$

![[Pasted image 20260408133335.png|250]]

We have easy access to the unnormalized version of the posterior distribution:
$$
p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})\propto p(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})p(\mathbf{z})=p(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x}) \triangleq \tilde{p}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})
$$
We can use $\log \tilde{p}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})$ to draw samples from $p(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})$.

### Sampling from $\tilde{p}$
#### Rejection sampling
Construct *proposal distribution* $kq(z)\ge \tilde{p}(z)$.
- Draw sample $z\sim q(z)$
- Draw height $u\sim \text{Uniform}(0,kq(z))$
- Reject sample $z$ if $u>\tilde{p}(z)$

![[Pasted image 20260408135836.png|250]]
- Requirement: we are able to evaluate $\tilde{p}$ at a given point $z$

#### Importance sampling
Does not provide a mechanism for drawing samples from the posterior $p(z)$, but it allows to compute expectations with respect to it.

Require proposal distribution $q$
$$
\mathbf{E}_{p(z)}[f(z)]=\int f(z)p(z) \, dz =\int f(z) \frac{p(z)}{q(z)} q(z)\, dz 
$$
$$
\approx \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}f(z^{(s)}) \frac{p(z^{(s)})}{q(z^{(s)})},\quad z^{(s)}\sim q(z)  \, 
$$
![[Pasted image 20260408150112.png|250]]
The ratio $\frac{p(z^{(s)})}{q(z^{(s)})}$ is the importance of each sample.

Set normalization constants such that
$p(z)=\frac{1}{Z_p}\tilde{p}(z)$, $q(z)=\frac{1}{Z_q}\tilde{q}(z)$

We can derive
$$
\mathbf{E}_{p(z)}[f(z)]\approx \frac{Z_q}{Z_p} \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S} f(z^{(s)}) \frac{\tilde{p}(z^{(s)})}{\tilde{q}(z^{(s)})} \approx \sum_{s=1}^{S} f(z^{(s)}) \frac{\tilde{r}^{(s)}}{\sum_{j=1}^{S} \tilde{r}^{(j)} \, } \,  =\sum_{s=1}^{S} f(z^{(s)})w^{(s)} \, 
$$
since $\frac{Z_p}{Z_q}\approx \frac{1}{S}\sum_s \tilde{r}^{(s)}$.

### Problems with rejection and importance sampling

- Rejection and importance sampling scale badly with dimensionality
- Proposed samples are independent and no information is passed from good/bad samples

## Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithms

Intuition: Allow information passing from previous sample.

- Construct a Markov chain having a stationary distribution $p^*(\mathbf{z})$ from which we can sample from
- Require $p^*(\mathbf{z})$ to be invariant, a sufficient (but not necessary) condition for this is that the Markov chain is reversible
- Require the Markov chain to be ergodic

### Metropolis algorithm
Start with some initial state $\mathbf{z}$ and iterate the steps:
- Perturb variables using proposal distribution $q(\mathbf{z}'|\mathbf{z})$, eg., $q(\mathbf{z}'|\mathbf{z})=\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{z}'|\mathbf{z},\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I})$
- Accept proposal with probability $\min\left( 1, \frac{\tilde{p}(\mathbf{z}')}{\tilde{p}(\mathbf{z})} \right)$

### Metropolis-Hastings algorithm
A more general version of the Metropolis algorithm (no Gaussian assumption).

- Propose a move from the current state $\mathbf{z}'\sim q(\mathbf{z}'|\mathbf{z})$
- Accept proposal with probability
$$
\min\left( 1, \frac{\tilde{p}(\mathbf{z}')q(\mathbf{z|z}')}{\tilde{p}(\mathbf{z})p(\mathbf{z'}|\mathbf{z})} \right)
$$

Since the Gaussian is a symmetric function, i.e. $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{z}'|\mathbf{z}, \sigma^2) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{z}', \sigma^2)$, when $q(\mathbf{z}'|\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{z}'|\mathbf{z}, \sigma^2\mathbf{I})$ we get back the Metropolis algorithm:
$$
\min \left(1, \frac{\tilde{p}(\mathbf{z}') \cancel{q(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{z}')}}{\tilde{p}(\mathbf{z}) \cancel{q(\mathbf{z}'|\mathbf{z})}}\right) = \min \left(1, \frac{\tilde{p}(\mathbf{z}')}{\tilde{p}(\mathbf{z})}\right)
$$
