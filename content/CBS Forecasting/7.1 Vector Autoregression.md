## Multivariate autoregression
Multivariate autoregression is a Vector AutoRegression (VAR)
In an $N$-variable vector autoregression of order $p$, $VAR(p)$ we estimate $N$ equations. 

In each equation we regress the relevant left-hand side variable $p$ lags of itself and $p$ lags of every other variable.

In a system of 2-variable regression of order 1, we have 2 equations, one for each variable
$$
\begin{align}
y_{1t}=\psi_{11}y_{1t-1}+\psi_{12}y_{2t-1}+\epsilon_{1t} \\
y_{2t}=\psi_{21}y_{1t-1}+\psi_{22}y_{2t-1}+\epsilon_{2t} \\
\end{align}
$$
where the disturbances, $\epsilon_{1t},\epsilon_{2t}$ may be correlated
$$
cov(\epsilon_{1t}, \epsilon_{2t})=\sigma_{12}.
$$

## Granger Causality
Given a model 
$$
Y_t=\beta_0+\beta_1 Y_{t-1}+\cdots+\beta_p Y_{t-p}+\delta_1 X_{t-1}+\cdots+\delta_q X_{t-q}+\varepsilon_t
$$
test if $X$ is useful for predicting $Y$.

We test the null hypothesis that coefficients on lagged $X$'s are equal to zero all together, that is $H_0:\delta_1=\cdots=\delta_q=0$.

We use $F$-test for that.

## Impulse response function
The impulse response function is a tool that helps us to learn about the dynamic properties of vector autoregressions of interests to forecasters.
>[!example]+ Housing starts and completions
>We will observe a shortage in housing. Thus, we give subsidies for new construction which should affect housing starts.
