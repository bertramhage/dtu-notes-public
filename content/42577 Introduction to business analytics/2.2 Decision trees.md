## Overall and metrics

**Entropy** measures the purity of a leaf and is computed as
$$
E=\sum_{k=1}^{K}-p_k\log_2(p_k)  \, 
$$
where $p_k$ is the probability of a certain class $k$.

**Gini index** is another common splitting rule
$$
Gini=1-\sum_{k=1}^{K}p_k^{2}  \, 
$$
$$
Gini_{Tree}=\sum_{l=1}^{L} p_lGini_l \, 
$$
where $p_l$ is the probability of a certain leaf and $Gini_l$ is the Gini index for a specific leaf.

**Approach**:
- Start at root node and find the feature and threshold with the lowest gini index
- Continue down the leaves: if gini index = 0 stop, otherwise, again, find the feature and threshold with lowest threshold
- Continue like this

## Ensemble methods
A group of *weak learners* come together to form a *strong learner*.

![[Pasted image 20250929090444.png|400]]

**Bootstrapping**: sample from dataset *with replacement*.
**Boosting**: sequentially train weak learners, each focused on *correcting the mistakes* of the previous learner, by increasing weights of misclassified data points.

### Random forrest
Enhancement over basic decision trees with bagging.
1. Build a bootstrapped dataset
2. Remove some *features* from the dataset
3. Build a decision tree classifier on the dataset

Efficient on large datasets, robust to missing information.

### Gradient boosting
Use small trees and has a learning rate to reduce overfitting.
1. Start from a naive prediction based on the training data
	$$
Odds = \ln\left( \frac{p(y=1)}{1-p(y=1)} \right) \implies p(y=1)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-Odds}}
$$
2. Compute the residual error between the observed and predicted
	$$
residual = y_i-p(y=1)
$$
3. Build a decision tree to predict deviation
4. Obtains new prediction by using the output of the new tree to correct the initial probability
$$
X_i=\frac{\sum residual_i}{\sum prev.probability_i \times (1-prev. probability_i)}
$$
$$
Odds = \ln\left( \frac{p(y=1)}{1-p(y=1)} \right)+0.1X_i
$$
5. Repeat

Eg. AdaBoost, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost
