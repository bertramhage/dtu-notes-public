## Components of a time-series

- **Trend**: A long-term upwards or downward movement in the data, indication a general increase or decrease over time
- **Seasonality**: A repeating pattern in the data that occurs at regular intervals, such as daily, weekly, monthly, or yearly.
- **Cyclical**: A pattern in the data that repeats itself after a specific number of observations (unspecified time interval), which is not necessarily related to seasonality
- **Irregular fluctuations**: Random fluctuations in the data that cannot easily be explained by trend, seasonality, or cycle.

## Time-series decomposition

**Additive decomposition**
When variation is *constant*
$$
y_t = S_t+T_t+R_t
$$
**Multiplicative decomposition**
When variation *changes with time*
$$
y_t=S_t\times T_t\times R_t
$$

## Stationarity
A stationary time series is one whose statistical properties are constant over time
- **Strong stationarity**: Joint probability distribution of a sub-sequence of variables does not change over time; i.e., joint distribution of $y_t,y_{t+1},\dots,y_{t+n}$ is exactly the same as the joint distribution of $y_{t+k}, y_{t+k+1},\dots,y_{t+k+n}$.
- **Weak stationarity**: Same mean and variance at all time points, and the covariance between variables at any two time points $t$ and $t-k$ (autocovariance) depends only on $k$ and not the location of points in time

![[Pasted image 20251020091740.png|450]]

### Checking for stationarity
- Visualize (plot)
- Compare distributions in different time windows of dataset
- Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) test
- Autocorrelation function (ACF)

### Handling non-stationarity

**Trend adjustment**: Eliminate the trend

1. Fit a regression model describing the trend component and then subtract it out of the original observations
$$
E(y_t)=\beta_0+\beta_1t
$$
$$
x_t=y_t-E(y_t)
$$
2. Differencing: simpler and does not assume a fixed trend throughout the time series
$$
x_t=y_t-y_{t-1}
$$
3. Transformations to stabilize variance (eg log. power transformations)

**Seasonality**
1. Seasonal adjustment: In the simple additive decomposition model, adjust each observation by how much the season mean tends to be below or above the global (stationary) mean $y_t-S_t$
2. Seasonal differencing: If $m$ is the seasonality, $y_t-y_{t-m}$ is the seasonally differenced data

## Autocorrelation

Generally, *autocorrelation* is the correlation of a signal $y_t$ with a lagged copy of itself $y_{t-L}$.

>[!info]- Partial correlation
>Recall correlation between two random variables $X$ and $Y$
>$$
>Corr(X,Y)=\rho_{XY}=\frac{COV(X,Y)}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y}
>$$
>
>For three random variables $X,Y,Z$ consider simple linear regressions
>$$
>\hat{X}=a_1+b_1Z,\quad \hat{Y}=a_2+b_2Z 
>$$
>and errors
>$$
>X^*=X-\hat{X},\quad Y^*=Y-\hat{Y}
>$$
>*Partial correlation* between $X$ and $Y$ after adjusting for $Z$ is defined as the correlation between $X^*$ and $Y^*$, $\rho_{X^*Y^*}$

Partial autocorrelation where the effect of intermediate lags is removed.
Thus, partial autocorrelation with lag $L=h$ is given by:
$$
pacf(L=h)=Corr(y_{t+h}-\hat{y}_{t+h},y_t-\hat{y}_t)
$$
$\hat{y}_{t+h}=\beta_1y_{t+h-1}+\beta_2y_{t+h-2}+\dots+\beta_{h-1}y_{t+1}$
$\hat{y}_t=\beta_1y_{t+1}+\beta_2y_{t+2}+\dots+\beta_{h-1}y_{t+h-1}$

- PACF allows to see "cleaned" effects of individual variables
- If the ACF plot does not dampen quickly, it is an indication that the time-series is non-stationary

## Modelling

### Naive methods
Good as benchmark
- Copy last value
- Trivial moving average

### Autoregressive (AR) model
An autoregressive model of degree $p$, AR($p$) is defined as
$$
y_t=\phi_0+\phi_1y_{t-1}+\phi_2y_{t-2}+\cdots+\phi_py_{t-p}+\epsilon_t 
$$
where $p$ is the auto-regressive lag parameter, $\phi_\xi$ are model parameters and $\epsilon_t$ the error term.

$p$ is a hyperparameter chosen from analysis of the PACF or trial and error / grid search.

### Moving average (MA) model
A moving average model of degree $q$, MA($q$) is formulated in terms of previous residuals (forecast errors) as follows
$$
y_t=\theta_0+\theta_1\epsilon_{t-1}+\theta_2\epsilon_{t-2}+\cdots+\theta_q\epsilon_{t-q}+\epsilon_t
$$
where $q$ is the moving average trend parameter, $\epsilon_\xi$ are residuals in previous time periods and $\epsilon_t$ the error term.

$q$ is a hyperparameter chosen from analysis of the ACF or trial and error / grid search.

### Mixed autoregressive moving average (ARMA) model
Combines AR and MA by *summing* the two models.

Thus has hyperparameters $p$ and $q$.

Summary of tuning:

| Model       | ACF                                      | PACF                                     |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| MA($q$)     | Cuts off after lag $q$                   | Exponential decay and/or damped sinusoid |
| AR($p$)     | Exponential decay and/or damped sinusoid | Cuts off after lag $p$                   |
| ARMA($p,q$) | Exponential decay and/or damped sinusoid | Exponential decay and/or damped sinusoid |

### ARIMA
$I$ component (which stands for "Integration") determines degree of differencing.

Archives new lags $y'_i$ for $i=t-1, t-2,\dots,t-p$

## Evaluating forecast accuracy

- Forecast errors: difference between observed value and its forecast
![[Pasted image 20251020114344.png|200]]
- Typically calculated on the test set and can involve 'multi-step' forecasts
$$
e_{T+h}=y_{T+h}-\hat{y}_{T+h|T}
$$
where the training data is $y_1,\dots,y_T$ and the test data is $y_{T+1},\dots$.
